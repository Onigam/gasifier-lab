# The 3D worlds

Five worlds built from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres, the one source; every constant tagged `reported`, `derived`, `estimate` or `third party`; what the simulation owns is imported from `src/sim/machine.ts` and never retyped: `dims.test.ts` holds that). `src/three/index.ts` exports `createFarmScene`, `createTractorScene`, `createGasifierScene`, `createGasTrainScene`, `createGarageScene` (all `lazyModule`s), `WORLD` (the `SceneDef` fields of each) and `PART_OPENS`. Ids as frozen in `docs/research.md` §4, one list in `src/three/part-ids.ts`: `farm` 10, `tractor` 13, `gasifier` 20, `gas-train` 17, `garage` 7, **67 in all**, each in exactly one world, each with meshes, a label in both languages and a side to be looked at from (`worlds.test.ts`). No text in any texture, no canvas, no file. **Not a build plan:** no cut line, no weld, no seal, no tolerance, no tool, no procedure in any of it (R1), and nothing here shows that a build would be acceptable or allowed.

**Geometry the model does not print (R2).** The throat, the nozzles, the firebox and the heights of the zones come from `NOT_PRINTED` of `src/sim/machine.ts` (`dims.ts` exports it as `HOT`, `HEARTH`, `ZONES`), are drawn to scale because a false model is no answer, and are printed nowhere: no label, hotspot or name carries a digit but the year 1940 and the two ends of the false-colour scale (`dims.test.ts`); no module formats one (a test reads the sources); the boxes of the parts that would give them away are empty on the part's sheet while the part is selected (`GASIFIER_REFUSED`: `throat-ring`, `air-nozzles`, `fire-box`, `reduction-zone`, `oxidation-zone`, `pyrolysis-zone`, `grate`, and `outer-casing`, which the lead added on 2026-11-11 after the browser showed its box on its sheet: the box is the lower casing's, whose height and diameter are listed with the hot parts' sizes in research §3), though the camera still gets the real one through `frameBounds`. **The sheets' « Encombrement »** is the kit's print of the selected part's box (three sizes, metres with two decimals from one metre up, else whole millimetres), so it can repeat a withheld length by chance or by construction: the lab's own review found about twenty such rows. Every world has a refused set (`GASIFIER_REFUSED`, `TRACTOR_REFUSED`, `TRAIN_REFUSED`, `FARM_REFUSED`; the garage has none) and the parts besides the hot ones that the guard found are in them (nine that repeat a withheld length, then ten that come within 3 mm of one, among them the chip column, whose half-cut bed is as wide as the casing it sits in), found by `encombrement.test.ts`, which prints the box of every part of the five worlds as the kit does, over 48 states of the model (the chips' level, the ash, the jar, the grate, the lid, the engine, the tractor's loop), and fails, naming the parts and no figure, if any figure of any axis equals a withheld length at the precision it is printed, or lies within 3 mm of one (`NEAR_MM`), in either language. A part that fails is put in its world's refused set; its sheet then has no « Encombrement » row. The guard also reads the kit's `envelope.js` and fails if the kit prints boxes another way. The ring's height and plate, the plenum, the hood, the grate's bars are estimates beside them and held to the same rule. The zones are drawn at the heights of the geometry, not at the journey cursor's shares (those are the simulation's, not to scale, on purpose).

## Frames

**Tractor frame T**, the machine's own, for every world: origin on the ground under the rear axle, x forward, y up, z to the tractor's right, so that its left, where the unit stands, is −z. **Unit frame U**: origin on the unit's axis at the foot of its lower casing, the same axes as T (no rotation): +x where the gas leaves, −x where the air comes in, −z the ash port and the left of the tractor. A world moves a root and nothing else. `farm` has its own frame F (x east, z south, y up), `garage` its room's (x across, z from the door, y up), `gas-train` G = T less the unit's place (the unit's axis at x = z = 0).

**Choices the geometry forced.**

- **The filter's place is derived**, by following the pipe from the unit's outlet: the outlet flange, a short run, a riser of 0.34 m, the tee (the fan's stack leaves up), the stop valve, the filter's inlet. `FILTER_AT` in `dims.ts` is that chain, not a typed number.
- **The mixer stands on the bonnet's top** (Fig. 3.8 draws it above the engine), through a riser, because the filter box fills the engine's left side from the heat shield to the front. The gas pipe from the cooler goes round the right of the bonnet to it; the water gauge stands on the bonnet's rear top, in front of the driver's eye, with a hose to a tap on the mixer.
- **The cooler's two ports are on the lower box** (Fig. 3.8): the gas goes up one half of the tubes, across the header and down the other. 108 tubes of 20 mm, 855 mm long give the reported 5.8 m² (derived: count and size are estimates); 13 flat bags of 0.26 × 0.72 m on frames give the filter's 4.9 m² (Fig. 3.6's flat bags, not tubes).
- **The bonnet is not a part.** It is a follower of `diesel-engine` that goes to the ghost whenever X-ray is on, the machine is apart, or the part selected is one of the four under it, so that the pump, the shield and the governor can be seen and leave without going through it.
- **A part inside the engine bay is read through what stands in front of it** (`seethrough.ts`, `IN_FRONT` in `tractor/scene.ts`; the lab's own answer to the kit's request 13, « X-ray while selected »). While the injection pump, the heat shield or the extra governor is the part selected, the engine (block, radiator, exhaust), the gas line and, for the shield, the pump go to the ghost, as in X-ray, and everything else stays solid for the context: the part reads as itself, with its outline, and not as an outline on the block's flank. The registry keeps what it shows (isolate hides, X-ray ghosts, the explode moves); `SeeThrough` only swaps the material of a part the registry shows solid and gives it back the frame the selection or the registry changes (tested).
- **The extra governor's V-belt** (`buildBelt` in `tractor/engine.ts`) is a closed band of a trapezoid section round the fan shaft's pulley and the governor's: two arcs and the two runs between them, redrawn from where the pulleys stand now, so that it stretches between them when the parts come apart and stays on both rims (tested: assembled and apart, to 2 mm). The governor stands a few centimetres behind the fan's blades and clear of the block's flank, so that the belt has room and the pulley does not sweep through the fan.
- **The hearth in section** follows Figs. 3.2a and 3.3: a straight fuel casing, a perforated sleeve in it, a gutter at its foot, the lower casing with the fire box in it (the hood joins its rim to the casing's wall), the nozzles on a ring inside the box's top, the throat ring a loose pot in the box, the grate over a pit of the model's ash.

## Attached and resting

Tested (`machine.test.ts`, `worlds.test.ts`): each wheel's lowest point is the ground within 3 mm (and the rear tyres are 1.76 m round and 0.467 wide); the sump is the lowest of the body (the clearance); the unit's base ring on the console's pad, the filter box on its cantilevers, the fan's plate on its shelf, the gauge and the mixer on the bonnet's top; the hot pipe's flange on the unit's outlet, the stop valve between the tee and the filter, the stack on the tee, the air valve on the unit's intake, every pipe's two ends on its two ports (tractor and train); the cooler's arms to the front bolster; the grate and its crank on one axis; the lid on its spring, the rod through the yoke; the cylinder's piston, rod and crank: the rod's length is constant and the crown never reaches the head over the whole cycle; the valves open in their strokes only; nothing of the cylinder lies on the near side of its cut (the pilot's jet apart: fluid in the plane). In `gas-train` every piece stands on the floor or on a frame that does. In `farm` the buildings stand on land that is flat to 0.3 m under them, every prop on the ground within 6 cm, every tree on `terrainHeight`. In `garage` the tractor stands on the floor, 5 cm and more off every wall, the ceiling and the rack.

**Exploded, along sensible lines** (`worlds.test.ts`): no part of a machine world goes through the floor; the unit's parts leave upward or out of the open side (never down through the stand, never through the cut half); the wheels go out along their axles; the line goes up as one block, the cab and the rack up, the left consoles out, the unit up and out; in `gas-train` the line spreads along itself about its middle, the bags go up, the vessel out, the throttles up, and the pipes stretch between their ports. Hidden parts leave through the ghost bonnet. Isolating one part hides the scenery of the others (tested).

## Section

The unit's section is **geometry cut once**, when it is built, along the plane z = 0, the far half (z ≥ 0) kept, each solid closed with a real face (`common.ts`: `latheCrisp`, `capPolygon`, `wallCap`): no clipping plane at draw time, nothing to z-fight, a click goes where the eye goes. The cut face of the bed is coloured by a shader that reads the field by height; the walls' cut faces are one pale colour, as every section is. The cylinder of `gas-train` is cut the same way, across the crank's axis, so that the piston, the rod and the valves stand in profile. `garage` is not cut: its walls step aside by themselves (`scene.onBeforeRender`: a wall is not drawn when the camera is outside it on that side, but the front wall, which holds the door, until the camera faces it).

## The unit seen from outside

The unit must not read as a drum (research R8; docs/qa.md D14), and it must not tell a size that is withheld: everything below is added to the casings **at their own radii and joints** (`UNIT` and `FITTINGS`: nothing moved, nothing resized, no height or width of the hot parts used or printed), and every part id keeps its meshes. The two casings have two finishes, so that they read as two bodies bolted together and not as one rusty cylinder: the fuel casing is grey-green paint, worn at the seams and chipped (`upperCasing`), the lower one dark scaled steel, blued here and there, with soot above the hot places (`hotCasing`, `scaleSteelMap`). Each has the seam of its rolled plate, a weld bead up the side. Where a pipe goes through the lower casing (the gas outlet, the air intake) there is a round reinforcing pad bolted on round it, as on a fabricated vessel (`pad`). The ash door stands proud of the wall on its frame, a steel plate bolted round its edge with its hinge knuckles on the gas side and its latch and bar handle on the other. The grate's shaft leaves through a gland and the bearing it turns in is carried by a plate bolted to the casing and an arm (the bearing stood on nothing before), and its crank ends in a ball. The lid has a carrying handle under the yoke. What is on the research's figure stays what it was: the clamp band and its eccentric lock at the joint, the lid on its spring, the drain with its hose and jar, the base ring and its bolts. The reduction taper is inside the hood, as in Fig. 3.3, and is left there: drawing it outside would change the shape the research draws.

## The hot parts in the section

The false colours (below) are the bed's: the cut face of the chips is bright, from blue to red. The metal that stands in the fire is not drawn in them: the throat ring, the plenum and the nozzles, the fire box's wall and hood, the grate's bars and its shaft are dark steel (`hotMetal`), their cut faces too (`hotCut` in `unit/unit.ts`: not the pale face every other wall of the section has), and they take the fire's glow from the same field as the bed, a cherry red from about 520 °C that turns orange only near the top of the ramp (`incandescence` in `unit/field.ts`). So in the section a hot part is a dark shape with red heat in it against a bright field, and isolated on the cream paper it is a dark part: the glow tells it is hot, its colour does not melt into the bed's (docs/qa.md D4: it was the same orange). One nozzle lies in the plane of the cut, on the air's side where the intake feeds the plenum, and is cut along its axis: two walls and its bore, from the plenum to the tip, with the air's dots running down it in « Flux »; the other nozzles stand behind the bed's cut face, as they did.

## The false colours

`unit/field.ts`. One ramp, the lab's (`heatStops(20, 1400)`, blue cool to red hot), fixed: a colour means the same at any moment and in every view. A profile through the four zones' temperatures (ten knots, in the unit's own frame) is the field; the bed's cut face, the ring, the box, the nozzles and the grate read it by their height in the unit, so a part lifted by the explode keeps its colours. The curved surfaces carry the fire's own light instead (incandescence over about 550 °C), so that the unit looks hot without the legend. The scale is a bar on a foot beside the unit, 20 to 1,400 °C, with two labels that are the ramp's ends and no size.

## Motion that reads right

Wheels roll at the ground speed the model gives (0.36 of a turn a second at the rear, 0.8 at the front: under the cap by construction, and tested). What turns faster (the fan, the pulleys, the governor, the starting fan's impeller) turns at most 1.2 times a second under a blur disc that fades in from 3 turns a second. The cylinder turns at 6 % of the engine's true speed, never above 1.2 turns a second: at 31 turns a second the eye would see nothing. Direction comes from the flow that drives it: the wheels turn forward, the fan sucks, the grate's crank goes with its shaft. `update` is the only thing that moves a world; `dt` = 0 moves nothing; every motion takes `view.slowMo`.

## Flows (« Flux »)

`flowpaths.ts`, `train/flow.ts`. Dots along sections, one path each, rebuilt from where the pieces stand now: in the unit the gas goes down through the throat and the bed, through the grate, out under the box, up the jacket to the outlet; the air in at the intake, round the plenum, to each nozzle and into the fire; the vapours of the drying through the sleeve to the cold wall and down to the drain. On the train: the hot pipe and the stop valve, the filter's dirty side, through the cloth, up the bag and out, the pipe to the cooler, up one half, across and down the other, to the mixer (the gas through the throttle, the air from the cleaner), the manifold, the exhaust; and the primary air from the fan's eye through its pipe and the valve into the unit. A dot's colour is the gas's temperature on the ramp 20 to 400 °C; the air is pale blue. They stand still, in their places, while the engine is stopped, and they are not drawn while the parts are apart (they would hang in the air where the parts were). A dot that is not shown has no size and waits on the start of its path: the kit measures a part by every vertex it holds, and a dot parked far away framed the void. In `garage` « Flux » shows the model's carbon monoxide as dots through the room (`docs/water.md`). In `farm` « Flux » runs the fuel's way: the logs to the chipper, the chips to their heap, through the sieve to the barn, from the barn to the rack on the cab (moving with the tractor), and the ash to its bin and the black water to its barrel, the density of the dots following the load (`gasFlowShare`) and never as a plan to do any of it (no person, no gesture). In `gas-train` the Sankey (`gastrain/sankey.ts`) lies on the floor in front of the line: ribbons of the model's own kilowatts (wood in, gas on, the diesel joining it, the shaft's work out, two grey ribbons of losses), 1 cm of width 2 kW, no figure.
