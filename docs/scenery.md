# The setting of the worlds

**Sweden, around 1970, a large farm in the south, in late June.** No source gives any of it (research §3: « farm, garage, room above: no source »): the buildings, the land and the day are estimates, drawn at the scale of a farm that could run a 5-tonne tractor on beets and grain over some hundreds of hectares (K38). Sizes that matter are in `src/three/dims.ts` (`FARM`, `GARAGE`), tagged. No texture file: every map is procedural (`src/three/textures.ts`), no text anywhere, because films are captured with no interface. Nothing here is a plan to build: the machine's own geometry is `docs/3d.md`'s.

## `farm`, the setting

A gravel yard under a low sun: the red farmhouse with its white corners and its porch, the garage and the bedroom over it on its west gable (the side away from the unit, as in `garage`: film round 3), the chip barn with its doors open on a heap of dry chips, the drum chipper and the sieve where the fuel begins, the woodpile along the barn's end, the ash bin and the barrel of black water by the garage's free corner. South of the yard a drainage ditch, a track on a fill across it, and the big ploughed field where a tractor goes round with its crusher at the model's speed.

**The land.** One mesh on a tensor grid of about 100,000 vertices: 2.5 m near the yard, 0.4 m across the ditch, to hundreds of metres at the horizon. The ditch is cut into the land (a flat bottom, banks of 1.7 m run to the metre); a track crosses it on a fill. The surface is computed per pixel from the position: gravel with ruts, ploughed soil with furrows along the field and a paler headland, meadow, wet dark banks. Every building stands on land that is flat to 0.3 m under it (tested), with its plinth sunk in the ground.

**The spruces by distance.** A spruce with its hundred and five thin boughs is what the picture shimmered with on a slow orbit and a dolly (docs/qa.md D16; the far spruces and the leaf cards were not the cause: hiding them changed nothing). Beyond `SPRUCE_COARSE_FROM` (28 m) from the eye a spruce is drawn as a stack of drooping skirts with a star-shaped hem and a dark lip under each (`spruceCoarse`), of the same height and reach: two instanced meshes, each tree in one of them by its distance, chosen each frame in `onBeforeRender` of the crowns (the eye's camera only: the shadow pass has none), with a margin of 12 % either way so that none flips at the limit (`lod.test.ts`). Near, the boughs; from the yard's overview, the skirts, which read as spruces at 25, 35 and 60 m.

A crown is made of leaf cards (alpha-tested sprays of leaves on a transparent ground) on the limbs' ends and through an ellipsoid, each spray lit by the crown's own normal, so that a tree reads from every side. **A spray is a lobed blob of leaves, not the card's square** (the leaves' centres stay within a lobed outline, so that the corners of a card are clear at every level of its mip chain), **and no limb ends outside the crown** (a limb is shortened until its end is inside the ellipsoid, and the spray it carries is drawn in): from above, at long range, a spray alone on a twig over the crown was a dark rectangle on a stick (film round 2's review, F1; looked at from 15, 25, 32, 40 and 55 degrees in round 3). From afar (between 26 and 75 m from the eye) a crown's leaves go to the crown's mean green: the leaf-by-leaf tones that a pixel can no longer hold are what glittered on a slow move. The card's mip chain is built by the lab (`leafMips`) and not by the GPU: a box filter carries the card's mean alpha to the coarse levels, over the alpha test, and a distant card passes as a solid rectangle (the dark rectangles floating over the birches of the farm's home view, found in the review of film round 1); each level from eight texels a side up now keeps the share of leaf the base has, spread, so that a crown is the same crown at every distance and at every angle of the card. Every tree stands on `terrainHeight` and keeps off the buildings, the field and the lines from which a part of the yard is looked at (`blocksSight` in `farm/scene.ts`: 7 m either side of the line from the part along its viewing direction, out to 70 m; a test holds it): a click on the house brings the camera to a house, not to a crown.

**The buildings.** The farmhouse: stone plinth, red boards with white corner boards and a belt between the floors, windows of white frame with a cross of mullions, curtains behind dark panes, window boxes with flowers, a porch on four posts with stone steps, a bench, a brick chimney with its cap, a grey sheet roof with its barge boards. The garage is the building of the `garage` world, roofed (`garage/building.ts`): rendered walls, a pair of board doors with iron straps, the bedroom over it. The chip barn: stone plinth, red boards, white trim, a loft hatch under a hoist beam with its pulley, a lean-to on posts, doors that slide on a track and stand open, a roof of clay tile with a ridge vent.

**The yard's props, each from its own function.** The chipper: a trailer chassis with its wheels and fenders, a towbar and its eye, a housing round a drum with knives (the drum is a part that turns), a feed trough with two spiked rollers at its mouth, a chute that throws, a PTO shaft with its guard, logs by the trough and one halfway in. The sieve: a drum of wire mesh on rollers in an angle-iron frame, an infeed hopper, a hopper under with its spout and a sack of fines, a chute for what stays in, a motor and its belt. The crusher: two gangs of 24 rings on an axle across a frame, a scraper, an A-frame pole. The ash bin on its slab, its lid ajar; the barrel of black water on two battens, its staves, three hoops, a bung and the black that has run from it. The woodpile: 600 logs in three stacks under a roof of boards. A drystone wall, post-and-rail fences, a wheelbarrow, a ladder on the barn, bales, pallets, tyres, a clothesline with its washing, a letter box. None of them shows a person or a gesture of use.

## `tractor`, `gasifier`, `gas-train`, in the open air

The three machine worlds stood on a paper studio (a warm floor with a grid, no horizon); a film shows them from the front and the eye needs a ground, its contact shadows and a horizon. They are now set in the farm's own yard, from one function (`src/three/outdoors.ts`, `createYard`): the farm's sky and its sun (the same Preetham sky, aimed along the side each world's key light always came from, between 30° and 52° up, with a shadow box a few metres wide so that a shadow's texel is a few millimetres), a ground of packed gravel going to meadow, a tree line of 60 large spruces in groves with gaps, two ridges at the horizon (curtains whose crest is transparent, a soft edge and not a cut line), and fog to the sky's colour. No paper, no grid. `tractor` rolls on a farm track: its gravel and the two wheel lines worn into it run under the wheels at the model's ground speed (the pattern scrolls, the wheels stay on their lines). `gasifier` and `gas-train` stand on a slab of concrete (its top is the floor, a few centimetres proud of the gravel) with its expansion joints, its stains and its worn rim; the slab is laid out round the line once the line is placed. Under each tyre a soft dark patch (the kit's multiplied decal) that follows the wheel along its axle when the machine comes apart; the stands and posts of `gas-train` are bolted down (nuts on studs), welded at their joints and gusseted. Every pattern finer than a pixel is faded with the distance (joints, stains, noise), or it crawls.

## `garage`, the room

The same garage as the farm's, cut open: the walls between the eye and the room step aside by themselves, the roof is left off, the bedroom stands on the ceiling slab. It stands in the yard of the farm (`outdoors.ts`): gravel, a concrete apron before the door that follows the room's width, a tree line and ridges at the horizon, and **the farm's own farmhouse** (`buildFarmhouse`, the same function, its roof stopping flush with the gable that the garage leans on) against its side wall. The house stands against the wall away from the unit, in this world and in the farm (the farm's garage was moved to the house's other gable in film round 3: seen from the door the house had changed sides between the two worlds). The house is not drawn when the camera goes round to its side. **Round the yard, a meadow with relief** (`outdoors.ts`, `meadow`): about 7,000 tufts of grass in patches (three crossed cards of blades each, lit as the ground, gone into the turf between 15 and 34 m from the eye) and 90 stones a third in the ground, none on the building, the house, the apron or the track; the ground's own picture has the blades near the eye, rounds of clover, drier turf, daisies, and **the track** the tractor took from the lane to the apron (two wheel lines of bare earth, the grass thin between them). Inside, the garage is lined with weathered upright boards over a tarred strip at the foot (a dark ground for the pale gas to be read against), the ceiling is boards on joists under a concrete slab (the cut edge of the slab is concrete), the bedroom's walls are pale lime plaster over a dark skirting. Seen from the yard (in the farm) the garage has the stone course the house has, a drip sill under each window of the bedroom, a gutter and its downpipe. The door: boards, iron straps, bolt heads along every strap, knuckles and pins at the hinge, the handle. In the garage: the tractor, nose in; a workbench with a vice, a can, a hammer, a spanner, a shelf of jars, a pegboard; two jerrycans and a pail; a bare lamp on its cord; a window high in the back wall; the ceiling's gaps, a hatch and a stove pipe through it (where gas could climb: the model's floor exchange is an estimate). On the left wall the 1940s rescue set on its board; on the back wall the CO indicator. In the bedroom: a made bed (posts with brass finials, a footboard, a quilt with stitches), a night table with a drawer, a knob and a lamp, a wardrobe with door panels, a seam, knobs, a cornice and a plinth, a woven rug with a fringe, a chair with a cushion and a rail, a window with curtains, a picture. Every window belongs to the wall that holds it and goes with it when that wall steps aside. **Nobody is in the picture** (R5): the bed is made, nothing is worn, no hand is drawn.
