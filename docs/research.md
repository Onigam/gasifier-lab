## A. Safety and law, first

This section comes before the plan because it decides the plan. Every role reads it before anything else.

### A.1 The plain rule

**A gas producer makes carbon monoxide on purpose.** About a fifth of the gas in its pipes is CO (K48), which cannot be seen or smelled (S5). It has killed people, and it has made thousands ill (K52, K53). The lab explains a machine. It is not a plan, a procedure or advice.

**No wording promises safety.** Banned in every visitor text, French and English, spoken and written, with a lint in `tools/`: _safe, safely, safe distance, safe level, harmless, risk-free, foolproof, non-toxic, clean gas, easy to build_; _sûr, sans danger, sans risque, sécurisé, inoffensif, gaz propre, facile à construire_. The one exception is a regulation or a body whose real name holds the word. The spring lid is a _relief lid_, never a « safety valve ». No statement says that a roof, a chimney, the open air, an open door, suction, a filter or a detector makes a gasifier acceptable. This file follows the same rule, outside the quotations at the end of §A.3.

### A.5 Law, per country

I am not qualified to classify a gasifier, and I found no text written for one at home. The lab says: **« such an installation, and any change to a vehicle, are subject to the rules of your country; ask your authorities before anything else »**, and nothing firmer unless a lawyer checks.

- **France, the vehicle.** A notable change to a registered vehicle requires a _réception à titre isolé_ from the DREAL network (S8, f; arrêté du 4 mai 2009 and arrêté du 11 janvier 2021 named there). The page names no gaseous fuel. **Whether a gasifier can obtain one I do not know, and I found no case.** Road tax, insurance and fuel excise: not checked.
- **France, a fixed installation.** The EU guide of 2009 reads the ICPE nomenclature as applying to the production of flammable gas (then rubric 1410) and to combustion of a non-standard fuel above 0.1 MW (2910) (S6 Table 3.3, t). **That table is 17 years old, and rubric 1410 has since been renumbered (from memory, not verified).** Légifrance not read. Below any threshold: unknown. The DREAL decides.
- **France and EU, the workplace.** CO limits 20 ppm over 8 h and 100 ppm short-term, binding (S5, t, France 2019; EU 2017). They concern workplaces, not homes, and are a reference for what the hazard is. S6 asks for fixed detectors with alarm at about 25/50 ppm in gasifier rooms, plus portable monitors, and for an ATEX zoning, for professional plants under 5 MW.
- **France, condensate.** Treated as effluent or waste under general law. I did not determine its waste code.
- **Switzerland.** « Yes; building laws of the Swiss cantons determine the authorization procedure » (S6, 2009). Vehicle approval by the cantons and ASTRA: **not checked**. Limit values at SUVA: **not checked**.
- **Germany.** Permit if the gas's thermal rating exceeds 1 MW (S6, 2009). Below that, and TÜV acceptance for a vehicle: **not checked**.
- **Sweden.** Notification below 10 MW, and a permit above 150,000 m³ of flammable gas a year (S6, 2009). The wartime regulations are chapter 13 of the Swedish book, **omitted from the translation** (S3 contents). A translation exists in Kjellström 1984, **not reached**.
- **UK.** « Needs to be discussed with the Environment Agency » (S6, 2009).
- **United States.** S4 is a federal manual; that says nothing about state road law.

No credit or reassurance comes from history: Sweden's own Research Council wanted all units under obligatory control, and in the first half of 1945 **155 of 230 vehicles inspected had faulty generators** (S3 p. 309, K54).

## 0. Summary and the plan against the bar

### 0.1 The machine

**A Swedish state institute's wood-chip gasifier on a 5-tonne farm tractor: gasifier type F-5, on a Massey Ferguson 1100 with a 5.8-litre direct-injection diesel, listed under 1968 and worked for more than 2,500 hours over six years on two farms in southern Sweden.** Tested at 40 kW it took **41 kg of chips and 2.2 kg of diesel an hour**, gave a gas of 5.6 MJ/Nm³ at 71 % cold-gas efficiency and 0.13 g/Nm³ of tar, and turned 18 % of what went in into work. Its bunker lasts about 45 minutes. Preparing it took 30–45 minutes a day. Its regular drivers would not use it, so agricultural students did.

Why this one (Q1): the National Swedish Testing Institute for Agricultural Machinery worked on it from 1957 because Sweden kept wood gas as its official emergency fuel. As far as I could find, it is the only small gasifier for which one public document gives **the dimensions, the whole system's schematic, an energy balance at three loads, tar and dust, a field record with service times, and failure rates**, with a truck run beside it as a check. Its reports are in FAO Forestry Paper 72 (S1, 1986), the Swedish originals unread. It has the roadmap's parts: hopper, drying, pyrolysis, oxidation at the nozzles, reduction, ash pit, cooler, filter, engine. The cyclone is on the programme's fabric-filter system (Fig. 3.5b) and not on this tractor's schematic (Fig. 3.8), and §4 marks it so. And it belongs to the past, built by an institution, which suits this lab.

The lab draws a **reconstruction**: no logo, the machine's name in Sources and sheet notes. It is an independent educational reconstruction, not a plan.

### 0.2 What the check of the sources changed

1. **The obvious source is the wrong one.** The FEMA manual (S4) is the best known gasifier document, with full dimensions, and it is a build guide from a galvanised bin and a mixing bowl. It reports **no measurement of gas, power or tar**, and says its unit « has not been widely tested » (p. 8). Choosing it would make the lab look like a how-to and leave the physics with nothing to compare. **Not the machine; used for a few cited facts only.**
2. **The roadmap's video is not a source of figures yet.** FoxTizZo has at least 31 episodes (the roadmap says more than 16), with measurements in episodes 17, 18 and 31 by their chapters or titles. No transcript was read, so nothing of his is in here (§1.6). His licence field is empty: standard YouTube licence, credit and link only.
3. **There are licensed videos, and one fake.** Cirad's and UVED's are CC BY from public bodies. One « CC BY » film is a re-upload that claims fair use: not a licence (§1.5).
4. **The reported numbers close to three figures, which is rare.** From Table 3.13 alone, with one derived value (wood at 17.1 MJ/kg) and the reported throat: the hearth load comes out within half a percent of the reported one at all three loads (§2.10; the figures are withheld from this file, R2). The bunker gives 44.5 min against the reported 40–50. **And the same table holds three figures that fail**: a 5.3 % efficiency that its own Fig. 3.11 shows as about 19 %, the total loss beside it, and an 18.9 % diesel share that computes to 11.8 % (conflicts 3, 4). I tuned nothing. The model must not reproduce the misprints.
5. **« Can an engine run on wood? » has an honest answer in three parts.** This one: **not on wood alone**. A diesel still needs a thread of its own fuel to light the gas: it keeps burning 15–20 % of the diesel it would have used (K20), and it gives 79 % of its power. A spark engine can run on gas alone at about half its power (K23, K24). And the wood is a heavy price: 221 kW in for 40 kW out (K16).
6. **The danger is not where people think.** Under way, the system is under suction, and a leak draws air in. **It is the stop**: the engine off, gas still forming, pressure rising, 23–27 % CO, in a garage, perhaps under a bedroom (K49–K50). « Some of the worst poisoning accidents in Sweden » happened that way (S3 p. 310). That moment gets its own world (Q7).
7. **FAO says « no permanent damage »; the French reference says otherwise.** INRS describes delayed syndromes and sequelae (S5). The lab follows INRS (conflict 10).
8. **Common numbers are wrong or blurred.** « Sweden's 70,000 wood-gas vehicles » were mostly on **charcoal**: at the peak, 36 % were on wood, about 26,800 of 73,853 (K58). « 40 % of the fleet » is not what the tables show (conflict 13). Reed and FEMA's « Jacques Imbert » is Georges Imbert in the authority files (conflict 14). FEMA's « by weight, 20 % hydrogen » is impossible (conflict 12).
9. **`sim/thermal` covers the casing, the cooler and the pipes, not the chemistry.** `sim/bio` is of less use here than the roadmap thinks (§5.6). What this lab needs is element balances and an equilibrium, which is small and pure.

### 0.5 The two guides

The modes stay, the frame is locked, and the standard's counts are met. **Both guides are history in the past tense**, about what the Institute's people did, never instructions. A lint rejects imperatives in FR and EN. Each opens with one sentence: this is a reading of published work, not a procedure, and nothing here shows that a build would be acceptable or allowed. **Every step ends on its origin** (_reported_, _usual practice_, _our reading_). No dimension, no method, no setting (R1–R4).

- **Assembly (20): how it went together.** FAO gives no sequence: **our reading**, from Fig. 3.2a and Fig. 3.8 with what §3.2.2 reports on the tractor. Mostly it says what each part is for and what sits on it. Steps §4.6.
- **Installation (10): from the chip heap to the end of a working day.** What was reported: chips sieved, loaded on the roof, preparation of 30–45 minutes, stops every 40–50, the work done, residues, the stop. Steps §4.6.
- **The mechanics still hold:** nothing floats at any step, every id of `gasifier` and `gas-train` is installed in exactly one step, and the tractor's consoles carry what they carry.

### 0.6 Sheets, lessons, spoken guide

**Sheets (A3, from `dims.ts`, estimates marked, « not for construction » in each title block):** 1 `general-arrangement` (tractor and system, the reported heights, areas and weights only) · 2 `gas-path` (the schematic of Fig. 3.8 redrawn from our parts, no sizes) · 3 `gasifier-section` (zones and parts named, overall height and bunker volume only; R2) · 4 `reaction-zones` (temperature and gas against height, model, reported bands marked) · 5 `energy-balance` (three loads, Sankey, misprints shown as such) · 6 `engine-power` (mixture energy; diesel, dual fuel and spark side by side, reported) · 7 `a-working-day` (Tables 3.17–3.18 as a calendar, and failures by hours) · 8 `co-ladder` (a logarithmic scale from 270,000 ppm in the gas down through exhaust, symptom bands and legal limits, each with its source and no « safe » side) · 9 `the-stop` (suction, post-gassing, a room at 50 and 1,000 m³, model) · 10 `war-and-fires` (Sweden's fleet and fires by half-year, redrawn from S3's tables).

**Lessons (two questions each):** 1 `the-match` (the flame does not touch the wood; what burns; gasification as a fire short of air) · 2 `the-gas-that-kills` (CO and blood; suction and the stop; 20 minutes; garage; 17 dead, 10,000 cases, 2,865 fires; what a smell can't tell you; why « outdoors » and « a detector » are not answers; **the reason this lab refuses what it refuses**) · 3 `journey-of-a-chip` (the four zones, the analyser, why reduction cools, why a throat, with no number) · 4 `what-the-engine-gets` (2,500 against 3,800 kJ/m³; why a diesel still needs diesel; 64 → 51 kW; a spark engine at half) · 5 `dirty-gas` (dust, tar, water; sieved chips and wet ones; hot in the filter, cold at the engine; cylinder wear; black water) · 6 `where-the-energy-goes` (221 kW in, 40 out; idling burns 55 kW for nothing; 3.6 kg of chips for a kilo of diesel) · 7 `what-a-day-costs` (45-minute stops, the rack, service per hour, why the drivers refused; why Sweden kept it anyway).

### 2.9 Conflicts between sources, and what the lab does

1. **Names in the tables.** « MF 110 » and « MF 1100 »; « F3 5/80-150 » and « F-5/80-150 ». The number 915 12763 and the 5.8 dm³ Perkins fit the 1100 (S10). I take MF 1100, F-5. Someone reading Axelsson 1969a could settle it.
2. **40 or 51 kW?** The energy balance's maximum is 40 kW at 1,855 rpm (Table 3.13, Fig. 3.11). The maximum power is 51 kW at 2,170 rpm (§3.2.2, Table 3.10). Different tests; FAO does not reconcile them, and speed alone need not explain it. The lab shows both, labelled.
3. **Part load's 5.3 %.** With 29 kW out of about 151 kW in, efficiency is 19.2 %, and Fig. 3.11 shows about 19 % there. The table's 5.3 % and 143.3 kW agree with each other and imply about 8 kW out. **I read them as misprints or as a different test. The lab uses Fig. 3.11 and says so on sheet 5.**
4. **Maximum's 18.9 %.** The diesel share computes to 11.8 %; part (14.9) and idle (26.1) match the table. Probably 11.9 misprinted. Same treatment.
5. **Throat not in the family table.** The reported throat [withheld: R2] is not a row of Table 3.2. The balance closes on it (§2.10). I take the reported value.
6. **Table 3.14's bunker « 0.031 m³ »** on a truck: not plausible beside 0.155–0.401 m³ in Table 3.2. Probably 0.31. Not used as a figure.
7. **Liquid and mass.** Table 3.10 prints 27 kg/h and 2.2 L/h for this tractor; Table 3.13 has 2.2 kg/h at 41 kg/h and 1.9 kg/h at 27. Table 3.13 is the fuller test.
8. **Moisture, wet or dry.** S1 ch. 2 uses dry basis, ch. 3 wet; S4 and S2 by weight. A 20 % wet chip is 25 % dry. Every moisture in the lab says which.
9. **20 minutes or hours.** S3 and S4: about 20 min. S2: « sometimes for hours ». S6: « quite some time ». The lab shows a slider and states all three. No figure is ever said to be when it ends.
10. **Are there lasting effects?** S1: « no chronic symptoms can occur », « no permanent damage ». S3: chronic poisoning, some brain damage, lasting mental disorders in « a small number ». S5: delayed syndrome after 7–21 days, reversible in 50–75 % of cases, sequelae otherwise; minor sequelae in 25–47 %; repeated exposure can have neurological and cardiac effects. **The lab follows S5.** It does not repeat S1.
11. **Exhaust.** S1 §2.8.4 calls it « acceptable, comparable to those of diesel engines ». S3: idling exhaust at 5–8 % CO, « considerably more dangerous » than a petrol engine's. The lab follows S3 and does not state a modern emission figure, which I do not have.
12. **« By weight. »** S4 pp. vi and 4 gives 20 % H₂, 20 % CO, 50–60 % N₂ « by weight ». Those are near volume figures: hydrogen at 20 % by weight is impossible here. Not repeated.
13. **« 40 % of Swedish traffic. »** S4 and the title page of S3's PDF. S3's own tables: 73,853 generator vehicles, about 30 % of the 1939 fleet, in a fleet that had shrunk, and most of those on charcoal. S3 p. 320: cars in use under 30 % of 1939. The lab gives the table. S4's « Denmark 95 % » has no source and is not used; its « millions » against S1 and S2's « almost a million » is not used either.
14. **Who.** S2 and S4 say « Jacques Imbert ». VIAF, BnF and the Deutsche Biographie give **Georges Imbert, 1884–1950** (S11; f, s). A Jacques Wolff wrote a book on him, which may be the source of the slip (my guess). Dates vary from 1920 to 1925: the lab says « in the 1920s ».
15. **Derating, rule and measure.** FAO's « 15–20 % » for direct injection against its own measured 21 % (MF), 29 % (Scania), and 10–38 % across the fleet. Model: `find`. Show both.
16. **Tar.** F family, 0.04–0.20 g/m³ measured. Imbert « usually 5000 ppm » (S2 p. 36), about 5 g/m³ by S2's equivalence. Other downdrafts 0.5–3 g/Nm³ (S2 Table 5-5). Updraft 5–20 % (S2). Not one number: the lab quotes this machine's and shows the band.
17. **Air and explosion.** S2 p. 120: air leaks under suction are « possibly causing gas explosions ». S1 §2.8.3: leaks into the gas system « do not generally » explode. Air burning hot gas near the grate can also destroy a unit (S4). The lab says: air and gas in a cold or closed part can explode. It rules on nothing else.
18. **Cooling's value.** FAO: a 10 % temperature reduction gives almost 2 %. S4: 1 % per 10 °F. Density alone: about 1 % per 3 K. Model: `find`.
19. **Air per kg of wood.** S2 Table 4-2 prints « 2.38 kg wood/kg air », surely inverted. A nitrogen balance on this machine gives about 1.8–1.9 kg air/kg wood (§2.10).
20. **Open air.** S1 §2.8.1 suggests the open air, « if necessary covered by a roof », and a chimney. S3 p. 312 found roofed and open-walled sheds wanting. **The lab endorses no arrangement.**
21. **« More than 65000 km ».** S1 §3.2.1, for trucks. Table 3.11's rows and the L8050 add to about 644,000 km (117,000 + 272,000 + 163,700 + 91,500). Probably 650,000 misprinted. The tractors' 15,000 h does add up (14,603 h, Table 3.10). The lab quotes the rows.
22. **A cyclone or not.** Fig. 3.5b, the programme's fabric-filter system, has one and a condensate vessel at the gasifier. Fig. 3.8, this tractor's schematic, has neither, though the text says the jacket drains to a vessel, so Fig. 3.8 simplifies. The photograph does not settle it. `cyclone` is in §4, marked.

## 5. Physics, for `lab-physicist`

**New local module `src/sim/gas/`**, with `sim/thermal` from the kit. No DOM. **Never tuned to §2.2.** The model reports its numbers beside them. Tests for the stars of §2.10. **Refusals bind the interface (R2, R4, R5):** the throat is a constant in `dims.ts`, not a slider. Hearth load and gas speed exist in code and tests, and never reach a panel or the strip.

### 5.1 The gas: one equilibrium, four balances

The method FAO cites (Tobler and Schläpfer, S1 §2.2.1), small enough for a browser. Wood CH₁.₄O₀.₆ (S2 Table 4-1). Unknowns: moles of CO, CO₂, H₂, H₂O, N₂, with CH₄ a fixed amount per kg of dry fuel. Equations: C, H, O and N balances; **water-gas equilibrium K_we(T) at a frozen temperature** (Table 2.2 from 600 to 1,000 °C); an energy balance with heat lost as a fraction, and gas leaving at its outlet temperature. Air or equivalence ratio comes from the energy balance. Outputs: composition, heating value, cold-gas efficiency.

`compare` with K13, K14, K46, K47; `find` against moisture (K59: is 40 % wet where the gas stops burning?). **S1 says this approach gives « reasonably good agreement »; check it does.** Sanity: all elements close to rounding; the gas holds no more energy than the wood.

### 5.2 The zones and the chip's journey

For the journey slider: a height coordinate, and for each height a chip state (temperature, moisture, char fraction) and a gas state. Bands from K47. Outer casing and firebox as a small `buildGrid('rz')` or RC network, with `surfaceLoss`, `radiationConductance`. **The reduction zone's profile as a first-order approach to the §5.1 equilibrium: `sim/bio`'s Arrhenius and first-order step, with parameters an **estimate**, since I read no rates.** If this adds nothing the visitor can see, drop it and interpolate, and say so.

Tar: **not predicted.** A measured band by load (K19, K61), with a label. Bridging and channelling: a stylised scenario, not a model. Packed-bed pressure drop (local Ergun, an estimate), `compare` with K60's fines.

### 5.3 The train and the engine

Gas outlet temperature from §5.1, the pipes by `tubeFlow`, `tubePressureDrop`. Filter: Table 3.4 as measured, linear, ageing by K63, ×6 when wet (K62), and wet whenever the gas falls below its dew point. Cooler: `effectiveness(UA, Cmin, Cmax, 'cross')` on K4's area, U an **assumption**. Condensate from the dew point, K64.

Engine: `power = n/2 · V · η_vol · (p/p₀) · (T₀/T) · H_mix · η` (S1 §2.1.2), H_mix from §5.1 (test: 2.44 MJ/m³), diesel, dual fuel or spark, the pilot as an energy flow. `compare`: K22 to K25, K28, K35, K36. Below K59's 4,200 kJ/m³, flag it. Air-to-gas ratio a slider, Fig. 2.3's fall on both sides. **No compression, timing or pilot slider (R4):** modes are presets of reported engines.

### 5.4 The room

Well-mixed box: `dC/dt = (S·x − Q·C)/V`, with V 50 or 1,000 m³, x CO fraction, Q air change. Sources: fan 200 L CO/min (K50), idling exhaust at 5–8 %, post-gassing **an estimate** with total volume and duration on sliders (no source, K49). Door shut, ajar, open: air changes per hour an **assumption, wide, flagged**. Output: ppm against the ladder (K51, K65), no person (R5). **One line said once: a real room is uneven and can be worse than this box.** Tests: the `exact` row. Also a second box for `room-above`, leakage factor an estimate. A model cannot show that any setting is acceptable, and the panel says so.

### 5.6 What `sim/thermal` covers, and what I'd share with `sim/bio`

**From `sim/thermal`, as is:** `airRho`, `airCp`, `airMu`, `airK`, `airPr` for the air path (hot gas needs its own properties, local); `createNetwork`, `stepNetwork`, `buildGrid` for the casing and firebox's thermal mass; `seriesU`, `surfaceLoss`, `flowHeat`, `effectiveness` for losses and the cooler; `tubeFlow`, `tubePressureDrop` for pipes (forward flow, so the open `tubeFlow` question does not arise); `radiationConductance` for the hot casing; `heatStops`, `heatRgb`, `heatRampGlsl` over 20–1,400 °C. **Not in it:** chemistry, gas mixtures, combustion, packed beds, engines, room dilution.

**With `sim/bio` (planned by the biodigester lab, a week ahead of this one).** The roadmap gives it « cinétique du premier ordre, loi d'Arrhenius, zones de réaction » for both labs. **I would share little: `arrhenius(A, Ea, T)` with one °C-to-K conversion, as the kit keeps °C; and `firstOrderStep(c, k, dt)`, exact and stable.** Perhaps a plug-flow march through zones, if the biodigester writes one. **Not** populations, pH, or acid-base. This lab needs **equilibrium and balances, not kinetics**, so it should not wait for `sim/bio`, and `sim/bio` should not grow « reaction zones » on this lab's behalf.

**A likelier shared piece, a small `sim/gas`:** heating value and stoichiometric air of a mixture, mixture energy, and a well-mixed room for a toxic or flammable gas. The biodigester needs the first three for biogas and the last for H₂S and CH₄; the mass heater might want the room for CO. Following the kit's habit, it starts in this lab or the biodigester's, and moves once two labs use it. **A request for `docs/kit-requests.md`, for the lead to decide.**

## 10. The film

Calendar: 31 December (`PLANNING` §4.2). Title is `video-writer`'s; `fact-checker` verifies. Working question: **« Can an engine run on wood? »**

### 10.1 Facts that could open a film or a Short

Each is true as far as the source given, and the wording matters.

1. **« Light a match and look at the flame: it does not touch the wood. »** The small gap holds gas leaving the wood, and that gas is what burns (S4 p. 4; t; general combustion knowledge agrees). A whole machine is that gap, made bigger, with the flame held back until the gas reaches an engine. _Strength:_ an opening on a phenomenon, slow, in close-up, with a question, and nobody needs a lab's name. Cite the fact from the physics, not the how-to. Do not use S4's « by weight » next sentence.
2. **« In the years of cheap oil, a government kept a tractor running on wood, on purpose. »** Sweden's testing institute worked on it from 1957. The tractor, listed under 1968, did 2,830 hours; one truck ran from 1969 for over 91,500 km and another reached 272,000 km, because wood gas stayed the official plan if oil stopped. In 1986 the responsible board still called it « the only realistic alternative » (S1 ch. 3; t). _Strength:_ a true, strange image, an insurance policy on wheels. _Careful:_ that policy is reported as of 1986; I do not know today's.
3. **« The regular drivers refused it, so they gave it to students. »** (S1 §3.2.2; t). The honest turn.
4. **For the middle of the film:** about 10,000 Swedes recorded with chronic poisoning in five years, and almost four vehicle fires a day in the first months of 1942 (K53, K56). With its second half: the machine's real threat begins when the engine is off.
5. **For a Short, the machine doing something (first-frame rule):** a tractor with a stove on its flank, stopped, a thread of pale gas leaving the air intake with the engine silent. **Reported:** white or yellow gas at the primary-air intake after a stop (S3 p. 309). What the plume looks like on this machine was not measured: the picture comes from the model.

### 10.2 Traps for the script

- **« It runs on wood »**: this one also burns diesel, 12–26 % of the fuel's energy. Spark engines run on gas alone at about half power. Not « free », not « clean », not « carbon neutral »: no source of mine weighs it. Tar and black water are real.
- **Sweden's « 70,000 wood-gas vehicles »**: most burned charcoal. **« 40 % of traffic »**: conflict 13. **« Denmark 95 % »**, **« millions »**: unsourced in S4. **« Almost a million »** is the sourced phrase, with no primary count. **Germany's 500,000**, France's 65,000: search summaries only, not used. **« Jacques Imbert »**: Georges.
- **Do not repeat any sentence of §A.3**, and **never show a gesture** (R3): no lighting, no flare, no lid. No « it's simple », no « a bin and a bowl ». A film can show a flame at the throat as a cut-away model. **« 17 dead »** is a span of 16 months, by a source I did not read, not a war total.
- **« No permanent damage »**: S1 only, contradicted by INRS. **« It only leaks when you stop »**: leaks exist anyway. Suction is a mechanism, not a guarantee.
- **The 18 %** is shaft out against wood and diesel in, at one test point. **The 51 kW** and **the 40 kW** are different tests.
- **The modern coda** (Cirad, C3, Viking): only after someone reads them.
