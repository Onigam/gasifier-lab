# Fact-check of the Gasifier Lab's public text

Checked 2026-11-11 on local `main` at `efb36f2`, by a checker who wrote none of it. **Report only: no file of the lab was edited** (`git status` is clean; every run below was a throwaway script kept outside the repository, importing `src/sim`, `src/data` and `src/panels` read-only).

**Verdict: not ready for the owner to read.** The warning every visitor sees first, the entry notice, the CO indicator's sheet, the guide's last step, the tractor overview and the lab's most important sentence about the stop each say something the sources do not: nine claim rows are WRONG or BLOCKED, twenty-eight more are WEAK. The figures themselves are mostly right (the whole CO ladder, the 20 / 100 ppm limits, 250 times, 20 minutes, 23–27 %, 200 L a minute, 5–8 %, 17 deaths, 10,000 cases, 2,865 fires, the phenols, the composition bands, the bench's 41 kg/h and 18 %, and every model value I re-ran); what fails is the reach of the sentences around them. Section 2 lists what to fix, in order, with the smallest rewording in French and English.

Verdict counts over the claim tables (section 3): 131 claim rows (some rows cover several places in the lab). **64 SOURCED, 17 SIMULATED, 10 DERIVED, 3 GENERAL, 28 WEAK, 6 WRONG, 3 BLOCKED**, 0 UNVERIFIED on their own (the one unverifiable point, the EU status of the 20 / 100 ppm values, is a WEAK row, H12). A row with two verdicts (« SOURCED / WEAK »: the figure is right, the sentence goes past it) is counted under the worse one. Section 2 lists 33 items, which group the WRONG, BLOCKED and WEAK rows by the sentence to change.

Words used for a verdict. **SOURCED**: a source says this at this strength (source and page given). **SIMULATED**: I ran the lab's own simulation in a scratch script and got this value. **DERIVED**: arithmetic on sourced values, redone. **GENERAL**: general knowledge, checked as stated. **WEAK**: the source says it only in part, or says less (how is in the note). **WRONG**: a source says otherwise. **BLOCKED**: no source at all. **UNVERIFIED**: I could not open what would settle it.

## 2. What must be fixed before release, in order

Each item gives the place, what is wrong, and the smallest rewording that makes it true. Tests that pin the old wording (`docs-guard`, `narration.test`, `data.test`, `refusals.test`) will need the same change.

### Rank 1. Said to every visitor, or about the stop, and not true as written

**1. « people who were being careful » (WRONG).** In `LAB_STRINGS.warningFull` (notice on the way in, Sources panel), `GAS_LINE` and the garage overview in `src/data/overviews.ts`, `README.md` (both languages). The one count of deaths the lab gives, S2 p. 119, says they died « because of careless gasifier operation ». No source says careful people died. The sentence is also the reverse of what the lab's own sheet 10 prints (« careless gasifier operation »). If the lab means that care is not enough, S3 p. 309 supports a different sentence: gas leaks out « even from a tight gas generator » (« même d'une machine qui paraissait étanche », which the notice already says).
FR: « … il ne se voit pas, il ne se sent pas, et il a tué des gens. » EN: « … it cannot be seen, it cannot be smelt, and it has killed people. »

**2. The notice on the way in: « certains des pires accidents sont venus d'un garage fermé, et une porte ouverte n'y a pas suffi » (WRONG).** `LAB_STRINGS.entryGarage`. It merges two statements of S3 that are about different things. p. 310: a filling lid that does not seal, air entering at the primary-air intake, the leak at the lid serving as the outlet, « particularly risky with a car that is put into a garage, for instance under a living room. Some of the worst poisoning accidents in Sweden were caused by such circumstances. » Nothing says « closed ». p. 311, a different paragraph: fanning, starting or idling with the doors and windows open, or the car pushed half out, is « far from satisfactory ». It is not said of those accidents. `docs/data.md` lists this sentence as resting on « S3 p. 310 »: p. 310 does not carry it.
FR: « À l'arrêt du moteur, le gaz continue de se former et de sortir, même d'une machine qui paraissait étanche : en Suède, certains des pires accidents sont venus d'une voiture dont le couvercle fuyait, garée sous une pièce où l'on vit. » (drop the open-door clause, or keep it only as in item 6.)
EN: « … in Sweden, some of the worst accidents came from a car with a leaking lid, parked in a garage under a living room. »
Same wording problem, WEAK, in: `parts/garage.ts` closed-garage and post-gas-plume `maintenance` (« garage fermé »), `sim/texts.ts` closed-garage lesson, `narration` `part-farmhouse` (the English drops « under a living room » from the claim and puts it in the next sentence: « counts a stopped engine among the country's worst accidents »; the French keeps it), `part-room-above` and the room-above sheet (« a car parked under a living room », leaving out the leaking lid). Add « avec un couvercle qui fuyait / with a leaking lid » to each.

**3. `install.ts` step `stop`: « the tractor went into the garage. That is where the Swedish book places some of the country's worst accidents… in a closed garage… Origin: reported » (WRONG tag).** No source says the institute's tractor was put in a garage; « closed » is not in S3. The guide's own rule is that every step ends on its true origin.
FR: « À la fin du travail, le tracteur pouvait rentrer au garage. Le livre suédois range parmi les pires accidents du pays ceux de voitures au couvercle non étanche garées sous une pièce où l'on vit. Origine : notre lecture. » EN: the same, « Origin: our reading ».

**4. `co-indicator` sheet: « no source we read describes an instrument of the period » and « Aucun détecteur de l'époque n'est décrit dans ce que nous avons lu » (WRONG).** S3 p. 313, under « Carbon Monoxide Indicators, Alarm Devices », describes the American M.S.A. indicators used by the Gas Generator Bureau (a portable model and a stationary one « intended for use as an alarm device… in car repair shops, warehouses, garages »; catalytic combustion, thermocouple, millivoltmeter in per cent by volume; readable to about 0.002 %), the Dräger indicator, an iodine-pentoxide method, a palladium paper test, and says no Swedish-made indicator was judged adequate. S2 p. 121 names the MSA « Mini (Peeper) » models (0–100, 0–500, 0–250 ppm; model IV with an alarm). The research's own part table calls it « period catalytic type ».
FR: « Le livre suédois décrit des indicateurs de l'époque, à combustion catalytique, dont un modèle fixe servant d'alarme dans les garages ; le cadran dessiné ici est de nous et n'imite aucun d'eux. » EN: « The Swedish book describes indicators of the period, of the catalytic type, one of them a fixed model used as an alarm in garages; the dial drawn here is ours and copies none of them. »

**5. « Nothing says when it ends » and « no end » (BLOCKED for the second, WEAK for the others).** Where: garage overview (both languages), `scene-garage` voice (« Rien ne dit quand cela finit »), lesson `the-gas-that-kills` body[2] (« Aucun chiffre ne dit quand cela finit »), `install.ts` `stop`, `post-gas-plume` voice (« Aucune source ne dit que ce gaz s'en va »), `panels/strings.ts` `roomHint`, and **`post-gas-plume.description`: « Il n'y a pas de fin : passé 20 minutes, la poussée n'a pas cessé, elle s'est affaiblie »**. The sources do give durations: S3 p. 309 and S4 §3.9.2, « the pressure increase… lasts approximately 20 minutes » and the unit « should be allowed to cool for at least 20 minutes » before garaging; FAO §2.8.1, gases are released « during a relatively short period »; S2 p. 121, « sometimes for hours »; S6 §5.4.4, « quite some time ». What no source gives is when a room is clear, or the real volume or the real duration on this machine. « No end » is the model's own choice (`docs/physics.md` §1.6, « exponential… no end »), written as a fact. `roomHint` also turns « sometimes for hours » into « plusieurs heures ».
FR: « Les sources ne s'accordent pas sur la durée (une vingtaine de minutes, parfois des heures) : aucune ne dit à partir de quand une pièce n'en garde plus. » EN: « The sources do not agree on how long it lasts (about twenty minutes, sometimes hours): none says from when a room holds none of it. »
`post-gas-plume.description`: replace the « Il n'y a pas de fin… » sentence by « Dans le modèle, la poussée s'affaiblit sans s'arrêter : c'est un choix du modèle, pas une mesure. / In the model the push weakens without stopping: that is the model's choice, not a measurement. »
Voice (`post-gas-plume`): « Aucune source ne dit… » → « Les sources ne s'accordent pas sur la durée, et aucune ne dit quand une pièce n'en garde plus : c'est la phrase que ce lab ne peut pas écrire. » / « The sources disagree on how long, and none says when a room holds none of it: that is the sentence this lab cannot write. »

**6. « The Swedes found open doors very unsatisfactory » (WEAK, nine places).** `scene-garage` and `garage-door` voice, `garage-door.description`, `farm-garage.maintenance`, lesson `the-gas-that-kills` body[2], q1 and its explanation, `ALARM_TEXTS.coDoorOpen`, strip alarm, closed-garage scenario lesson. S3 pp. 311–312 say it of **fanning, starting and idling** with doors and windows open (and of a hose through a window, and of open-walled sheds), not of a parked car's post-gassing. The lab uses it beside a model of the post-gas room. Say what the source says: FR « Les Suédois ont jugé très insuffisant de lancer le ventilateur ou le moteur portes de garage ouvertes » / EN « The Swedes found it far from satisfactory to fan or start with the garage doors open ». Strip: « Porte ouverte : jugé très insuffisant en Suède pour le démarrage / Open door: far from enough in Sweden, for fanning and starting ». The lab's reason for not saying the door helps is sound; the claim just has to be the source's.

### Rank 2. Health and hazard statements looser or stronger than their source

**7. « after-effects can return 7 to 21 days later » (WEAK).** Lesson `the-gas-that-kills` body[0]. INRS p. 6: after an acute poisoning there is « parfois » a pseudo-dementia state that « peut survenir après une période de rémission de durée variable (7 à 21 jours) », reversible in 50 to 75 % of cases after several months, otherwise with sequelae (25 to 47 % have minor ones). It is a delayed syndrome after a symptom-free interval, not sequelae returning.
FR: « …et, parfois, un syndrome tardif apparaît après une rémission de 7 à 21 jours ; des séquelles sont possibles. » EN: « …and sometimes a delayed syndrome appears after a symptom-free interval of 7 to 21 days; after-effects are possible. »

**8. « the body gives no warning » (WEAK).** Same lesson. INRS p. 5: the first signs (headache, nausea, vertigo) are insidious and can pass for food poisoning or drunkenness; 46 % of proven poisonings are said to be asymptomatic. The body does give signs; they are easy to misread.
FR: « on ne le sent pas, et ses premiers signes se prennent pour autre chose ». EN: « it cannot be smelt, and its first signs are easily taken for something else ».

**9. « Les Suédois ont compté 17 morts… selon un manuel américain » (WEAK).** Lesson body[3]. S2 p. 119 does not say the Swedes counted: it says « Seventeen people were killed in Sweden between December 1939 and March 1941 because of careless gasifier operation (Foley 1983) », a 1983 secondary work the lab did not read. FR: « Un manuel américain, citant un ouvrage de 1983, compte 17 morts en Suède de décembre 1939 à mars 1941, par négligence dans la conduite des gazogènes. » EN: « An American handbook, citing a 1983 work, counts 17 deaths in Sweden from December 1939 to March 1941, from careless operation of gasifiers. »

**10. The two researchers (WEAK).** `fuel-casing.description`, `assembly bunker.warning`. S2 p. 119: « two researchers at a Midwestern university died from CO inhalation when they climbed inside of a gasifier fuel bin ». It gives no mechanism: « a closed space holding wood and a remnant of gas » is the lab's own reading. Cut that clause: « Deux chercheurs d'une université du Midwest américain sont morts du monoxyde de carbone après être descendus dans la soute d'un gazogène. » / « Two researchers at a Midwestern university died of carbon monoxide after climbing into a gasifier's bunker. »

**11. The nine pellet-store deaths and « stored wood gives off CO » (WEAK).** `chip-barn` description, short and voice, `install.ts` `store`. HSE OPSTD 3-2012 (5 November 2012): « at least nine fatalities in Europe » since 2002 « following entry into wood pellet storage areas » (four ships' holds, two silos, three domestic stores). Three limits: the count is **as of November 2012** (« since 2002 » reads as to date); it is about **wood pellets in sealed hoppers, tanks and store rooms**, not chips in a barn or « a closed heap »; and it was read through a summary, which the public text admits (« source lue en résumé, à relire », in `chip-barn.design` and the Sources panel): it is now read in full and that note can go.
FR: « Le bois stocké peut dégager du monoxyde de carbone : une alerte britannique de 2012 compte au moins neuf morts, depuis 2002, après l'entrée dans des stockages fermés de granulés en Europe. » EN: « Stored wood can give off carbon monoxide: a British safety alert of 2012 counts at least nine deaths since 2002 after entry into closed wood-pellet stores in Europe. » Voice: « Et des granulés stockés dans un local fermé dégagent, d'eux-mêmes, du monoxyde de carbone. » / « And wood pellets kept in a closed store give off carbon monoxide by themselves. »

**12. The phenols (WEAK).** `condensate-barrel` sheet and voice, `field-ditch`. S2 p. 121: « the phenol content of **typical** gasifier or gas-cooler condensate is from 1500 to 3000 mg/L », against Sweden's wartime maximum for water released to sewers, « 10 g/m³ (10 mg/L) ». Both figures are sourced and 150 to 300 times is right. The source does not measure this machine's water, and the voice says « It holds ». FR (voice): « Typiquement, l'eau d'un gazogène contient mille cinq cents à trois mille milligrammes de phénols par litre. » EN: « Typically, a gasifier's water holds fifteen hundred to three thousand milligrams of phenols a litre. » Same word « typically » on the sheet. Also: « the main source says it has no hard data on what became of them » is FAO §2.8.4 « no hard data are available on the **bio-degradation** of the phenolic and tarry constituents of the condensates »: say « on their bio-degradation ».

**13. Hot ash « in the Swedish book » (WRONG attribution).** `ash-bin` sheet and voice, `ash-pit.maintenance`. I found no such statement in S3 (chapter 12 lists the causes of fire as sparks while filling, liquid starting fuel, insulation, engine heaters, electric faults). It is ORNL-6404 §3.9.3 (printed pp. 60–61): « Disposal of ashes must only be attempted after the unit has cooled down (to below 150 °F)… preferably hosed down with water ». ORNL is credited in the Sources panel for « four facts only »: this is a fifth. FR: « une source américaine range la cendre chaude parmi les risques d'incendie » / EN « an American source lists hot ash among the fire risks ».

**14. « French and EU limit… a legal line » (WEAK, partly UNVERIFIED).** Ladder rungs 20 and 100 ppm, `co-indicator` specs, `ALARM_TEXTS.coWorkLimit`. INRS p. 2: « France (VLEP réglementaires contraignantes – 2019) 20 ppm, 100 ppm short term »; « Union Européenne (2017) » the same values. France is binding, as stated. The sheet does not say the EU's are binding, and I could not open EUR-Lex or Légifrance (bot challenge and 404) to settle it; to my memory the EU values of Directive (EU) 2017/164 are indicative values that states transpose. FR: « Limite française (contraignante, 2019) et valeur de l'Union européenne (2017) sur 8 h, au travail ». EN: « French binding limit (2019) and EU value (2017) over 8 h, at work ».

**15. « il ne se pose pas et ne s'écarte pas : il reste dans la pièce » (WEAK).** `post-gas-plume.maintenance`. INRS p. 2 gives the density, 0.97 against air, and colourless and odourless: sourced. « Stays in the room » is the lab's inference, and the lab's own model has the figure fall from 3,498 to 687 ppm when the door opens. FR: « sa densité, 0,97 contre l'air, est presque celle de l'air : il se mêle à l'air de la pièce, et un garage le garde en couches (livre suédois) ». EN: « its density, 0.97 against air, is almost air's: it mixes into the room's air, and a garage holds it in layers (Swedish book) ».

**16. Idling exhaust, short forms (WEAK).** `exhaust-pipe.short` (« a wood-gas exhaust holds 5 to 8 % »), `exhaust-plume.short` (« 5 to 8 % comes out of its exhaust »), `ALARM_TEXTS.exhaustIndoors`. S3 p. 311: « the exhaust gases from an idling generator gas engine contain 5 % to 8 % ». The long forms say « wartime engines » and « no measurement on this machine »; the short ones drop both. Add « des moteurs de la guerre / of wartime engines ». The lesson `where-the-energy-goes` body[1] says the unburnt gas is « why the exhaust holds 5 to 8 % »: that cause is the model's (it sets the idle burn from that same figure, `docs/physics.md` §1.3), not S3's.

### Rank 3. Figures the voice or sheets say that go past their source

**17. « a Swedish institute counted two hundred and twenty-one kilowatts going in » (WEAK).** `intro` voice, both languages. The report gives 41 kg/h, 2.2 kg/h, 18.0 % and 181.5 kW of losses (Table 3.13). 221 is the lab's sum (194.8 kW of wood from the table's losses and cold-gas efficiency, plus 26.1 kW of diesel at an assumed 42.7 MJ/kg; 40 + 181.5 = 221.5 and 40 ÷ 0.18 = 222 are the table's own). The sheets mark it † and the voice cannot. FR: « Au banc, 41 kilos de plaquettes et 2,2 kilos de gazole à l'heure donnaient quarante kilowatts à l'arbre : dix-huit pour cent, dit le rapport. Soit, par le calcul, environ deux cent vingt kilowatts en entrée. » EN: « On the bench, 41 kilos of chips and 2.2 kilos of diesel an hour gave forty kilowatts at the shaft: eighteen per cent, says the report. By our sum, about two hundred and twenty kilowatts going in. »

**18. « Why the gap? The model's engine burns better than the bench's » (WEAK).** `part-wood-gas-tractor`, `where-the-energy-goes` body[0], `the-match` body[2]. `docs/physics.md` §3.3 says « two causes that add »: the engine (26.8 % against 24.3 % derived from the table) **and the gasifier** (75.5 % of the wood's energy in the gas against 71.1 %). I redid it: with the bench's 71.1 % the model would burn 36.5 kg/h instead of 34.3, so the gasifier is about a third of the 6.7 kg/h gap and the engine about two thirds. The voice names one cause of two. FR: « Le moteur du modèle brûle mieux que celui du banc, et son gazogène aussi. » EN: « The model's engine burns better than the bench's, and so does its gasifier. » (The order 34 before 41 is kept everywhere the voice says 41; only this segment says it.)

**19. « un pour cent de puissance en plus pour trois degrés » (WEAK).** `part-gas-cooler` voice only. The sheet is honest: three accounts (`gastrain.ts`). FAO §2.6 (introduction): « a ten percent temperature reduction… increases the maximum output… by almost two percent »; ORNL-6404 p. 14 (« for every 10 degrees above 70°F, an estimated 1% horsepower is lost ») is 1 % per 5.6 K. The voice gives only the lab's density-only figure, 1 % per 3 K, which is two to five times either source. FR: « Un gaz plus froid est plus dense : selon le rapport, dix pour cent de température en moins donnent presque deux pour cent de puissance en plus. » EN: « …according to the report, a ten per cent drop in temperature gives almost two per cent more power. »

**20. « a five-tonne tractor that runs on wood » and « It hardly drinks any diesel » (WEAK).** `part-wood-gas-tractor` and `intro` voice. The same part's sheet says « Wood alone is not enough »; the research's own list of traps says not to write « it runs on wood ». Substitution is 80–85 % (FAO §3.2.2), so 15–20 % of the diesel remains, and diesel is 12–26 % of the fuel energy. FR: « …un tracteur de cinq tonnes qui roule au bois, avec un filet de gazole ! » / « Il boit bien moins de gazole : il mange du bois ! » EN: « …that runs on wood, with a thread of diesel! » / « It drinks far less diesel: it eats wood! »

**21. « The only serious breakdown of the whole programme » (WEAK).** `part-cooler-condensate-vessel` voice. FAO §3.2.2: « One serious engine failure was experienced » **on this tractor**, probably from an overfull cooler vessel. Nothing is said of the programme. FR « La seule panne grave de ce tracteur vient probablement de lui » / EN « This tractor's one serious failure probably came from it ».

**22. « the honest answer is: not alone, and dear » (WEAK).** Lesson `what-a-day-costs` body[2]. FAO §3.4.5 finds a loss in Swedish conditions even with free wood (1984 US$), and an economic case at lower wages and wood prices. FR: « …pas seul, et, selon le calcul du rapport pour la Suède, à perte. » EN: « …not alone, and, in the report's costing for Sweden, at a loss. »

**23. « adds 450 kg to a tractor of 5,090 kg » (WRONG).** Tractor overview (FR and EN), `tractor-chassis.description`. FAO Table 3.12: « Total weight in producer gas version 5090 kg »: the 450 kg is inside it. FR « Le système de 450 kg fait partie des 5 090 kg du tracteur en version gaz » / EN « The 450 kg system is part of the gas version's 5,090 kg ». (« nearly nine per cent of its mass », 450 ÷ 5,090, is right.)

**24. « burn by half » / « half burns » (WEAK).** `gasifier-unit.description`, `chip-column.description`. The lab's own sheets and lesson say about 30 % of the wood burns (S2 p. 24: « Approximately 30 % of the biomass is burned »). FR « brûlent en partie faute d'air » / EN « burn in part for want of air ».

**25. « More than ninety-nine per cent of the tars vanish there: that is arithmetic » (WEAK).** `oxidation-zone` voice and sheet. The arithmetic is right (0.13 g/Nm³ × 2.17 Nm³/kg = 0.03 % of the wood). The « 99 % » compares it with an updraught gasifier's 5–20 % (S2), which assumes this machine's pyrolysis releases that much first: that is an assumption. FR: « Presque aucun goudron n'en sort : 0,03 pour cent du bois, au banc, contre 5 à 20 pour cent pour un gazogène à courant montant. » EN: « Almost no tar comes out: 0.03 per cent of the wood on the bench, against 5 to 20 per cent for an updraught gasifier. »

**26. Voice: « The published figures come from that institute's report and from a Swedish book » (WEAK).** `intro`. The figures come from an FAO report on the institute's work (the Swedish originals unread, as the Sources panel says), a Swedish book, and also an American handbook (the 17 deaths, « sometimes for hours », the phenols), INRS and others. FR: « Les chiffres publiés viennent d'un rapport de la FAO sur le travail de cet institut, d'un livre suédois sur les gazogènes de la guerre, et de quelques manuels. » EN: the same. Likewise « the institute's report / bench » throughout.

**27. 155 of 230 (WEAK).** Lesson `the-gas-that-kills` body[3]. S3 p. 309: 230 vehicles « inspected because of police reports; 155… 67 % ». The sheet and the spring-lid sheet say « after police reports »; the lesson does not, and so reads as a rate among all gasifiers. Add « contrôlés après des signalements de la police / checked after police reports ».

**28.
