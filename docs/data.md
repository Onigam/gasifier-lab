# Data of the Gasifier Lab (author role)

The texts of the lab, written in French and English together (`l(fr, en)`): **67 part sheets** (Overview, Specs, Maintenance, Design) for the 67 ids of `src/three/part-ids.ts`, two guides (**20 assembly steps, 10 installation steps**), five world overviews, **seven lessons with fourteen questions**, the Sources panel, the lab's interface strings, and the ten A3 sheets (`docs/drawings.md`). The machine is the Swedish F-5 wood gasifier on an MF 1100 tractor, rebuilt from a state institute's report. It **explains and gives no plan**: this is what sets most of the choices below.

## Conventions

- **Marks.** No mark: reported by a source and written in the text itself, from a list (`REPORTED`) where each number is tied to a research figure. `†`: computed from published figures, or the model where **this machine was measured** (the bench's three points). `*`: an estimate, or the same model where it was not measured (past 20 % moisture, in a fault, on another engine), and everything about the farm, the garage and the room above. A figure is written `{key}` and printed with its mark: a typed number with a `†` or `*` is caught by the test, and so is a number that is in no list.
- **34 kg an hour first.** What the model burns at the bench's maximum is said first and flagged, then what the bench measured (41); no text says the model retrieves the machine. The flame never goes out in the model, and no text says it does.
- **Said once.** « Independent educational reconstruction » and the warning in full: the Sources panel; the warning also in the garage's overview, with the one line on poisoning (« call your local emergency number »: the lab carries no number of any country), and in the notice on the way in. The farm's overview does not repeat the notice: it names carbon monoxide in one different line that points to the garage. « This lab explains, it gives no plan »: the farm's overview. « Not a procedure »: the first step of each guide. « Not for construction »: the title block of each sheet. The law: one plain sentence in the panel. The carbon monoxide's source is named in the panel.
- **The hot parts' sizes are printed nowhere**: the throat, the nozzles, the firebox, the grate, the zones' heights, the plenum and the hood, the lower casing. A text says what a hot part **does**, its life and its temperature. A test (`src/data/refusals.test.ts`) holds every printed form of every refused length, in either language, off every text of `src/data`, `src/drawings` and `src/i18n`, and holds that no module reads the hot geometry.
- **No procedure.** No tool, no torque, no duration in a guide step; a lint rejects an imperative of an operating verb at the start of a sentence (light, fill, drain, weld…). The guides are told in the past tense, as the institute did it, and the first step of each says so. No sentence tells a visitor how to build, light, run or make safe a gasifier. No disposal advice for tar, ash or condensate: what they contain, and « ask your local authority ».
- **Words that promise** (safe, harmless, clean gas, easy to build, and their French) and the source sentences the lab does not repeat (research §A.3) are held off every text by test.
- **Names.** A research reference such as `(K22)` never reaches a visitor. Sources are named in words in the panel and, on the ladder, beside each rung. S4 (the American emergency manual) is cited by title and number and not linked; the amateur's construction videos are credited without a link; no image or footage is taken from a video.
- **British English**, `draught`, `litre`, `metre`. The English is an adaptation, not a word-for-word translation; both languages carry the same numbers (a test compares them).

## Open questions

1. **The fires per 1,000 vehicles by half-year** (PL-G10 plans a curve): the research read it from a scanned table whose title says « per year ». The fact-check read tables 52 and 53 and settled it: the values are per half-year, the title is wrong. The two end values (about 20, about 3.4) are still in words, and no curve is drawn.
2. **Is « sometimes for hours » S2's wording?** Yes: Reed and Das, p. 121 (« CO continues to be evolved, sometimes for hours »), found by the fact-check. It stays in quotation marks on PL-G09.
3. **The 1 m³ the stop leaves, the buildings, the exchange of air through the floor.** None has a source. Every number of the room carries `*`, and the sheets say the model's room mixes perfectly, which the real one does not (it is uneven, in layers).
4. **The tar.** `tarCrackedShare` is a mechanism with rates from memory and a time the state never holds; the lab prints only the measured band (0.13 g/Nm³ at full power, 0.04 at part load, 0.17 at idle).
5. **The model's engine on the MF 1100** (55 kW against 51) is the one agreement worth little: the sheets say why (47 to 60 kW by the efficiency chosen, +30 % on the fleet).
6. **The jacket's size, the casing's surface and what the 15 % of loss is made of** are estimates: the casing's own surface loses about 12.5 kW* and the chemistry's `h` says 24 kW. They are said where they stand.
7. **S3 p. 260 and p. 309** give a ventilation (four air changes an hour) and a wait (twenty minutes before the car goes into the garage). No text repeats them: they read as a way to make it acceptable (R3, R5).
8. **No licensed footage.** Nothing is taken from a video.
9. **No emergency number, no national limit.** The owner's decision (2026-11-11): the lab is international and carries nothing that nobody could verify for every country. The warning, the garage's overview and the notice say « call your local emergency number », and the line « get into the open air, do not go back in » stays the lab's own wording of general first-aid advice, no medical protocol (R6); it must not grow. The ladder's rungs at 20 and 100 ppm say what the INRS sheet says (« occupational exposure limit value », 8 h and short periods) and that limits differ from country to country: no legal status, no EU scope.

## Blocking for release

None of these stops the lab from being `building`. Each stops it from being `released`, and none is for an agent that built the lab to settle: an independent fact-check reads the pages and the owner decides.

1. **S5 (INRS FT 47) and S2 (Reed and Das, 1988): the 20 and 100 ppm limits of the carbon monoxide ladder, and the rungs and bands around them.** Checked by the fact-check, all right. The EU's status of the values is no longer an open point: the ladder says what the INRS sheet says (« occupational exposure limit value ») and no more. The researcher and the author worked from a summary and a partial text (research §1.2, access **f**; `docs/physics.md` §9): the INRS sheet's occupational exposure limit values (20 ppm over 8 h, 100 ppm short term), the symptom rungs, the explosion limits, « sometimes for hours », the phenols and the 17 deaths. They stand on PL-G08, PL-G09, lesson 2, the garage's overview and the Sources panel. A figure of this ladder is the one a visitor might act on: it is read before release, page by page.
2. **S3 pp. 302 to 321: the fleet, the fires, the chronic cases.** The tables were read through an OCR text layer whose columns are scrambled; the rows were checked by adding (PL-G10), and again by the fact-check. The curve of the fires per 1,000 vehicles stays undrawn (open question 1). The 10,000 chronic cases are the FAO's (§3.6), not S3's.
3. **The one plain sentence on the law** (`legal` in `LAB_STRINGS`, which no lawyer has read). The lab carries no emergency number and no national limit (open question 9).
4. **The notice on the way in** (`entryGarage`, `entryFire`, `entryBurns`): three lines written by the lead from research §A.3. The fact-check found `entryGarage` and the warning's « people who were being careful » not true as written; both are reworded on the sources' words (a car with a leaking lid, parked in a garage under a living room; « it has killed people »), and the owner reads them again.

## After the fact-check and the review (2026-11-11)

`docs/fact-check.md` (131 claims: 6 wrong, 3 blocked, 28 weak) and `docs/qa.md` were applied to every written text: the sheets, the guides, the overviews, the lessons and the quiz, the alarms, the Sources panel, the notice on the way in, the README and the sheets. The spoken guide is the narrator's. What is worth knowing for the next reader:

- **The sentences go no further than their source.** « People who were being careful » is gone (S2 says « careless »); the worst Swedish accidents are told as S3 p. 310 tells them (a car with a leaking lid, in a garage under a living room), and « an open door was not enough » is said of what S3 pp. 311–312 say it of (fanning and starting). « Nothing says when it ends » became « the sources do not agree on how long it lasts, and none says from when a room holds none of it »; « no end » is said to be the model's choice. The 1940 rescue set is « of the 1940s », and no sentence says how common it was.
- **The kit's « Encombrement » is not data.** The kit prints it from the 3D box of the selected part; the only lever is the refused set of each world in `src/three` (`GASIFIER_REFUSED` in the gasifier's scene). `fuel-casing` has no dimension row any more (its diameter and its height would give a withheld size), the guard of `printedForms` reads « a × b m » (the first and the middle figures are followed by their ×), and `refusals.test.ts` now reads the dimension rows too, which `allTexts` leaves out.
- **The pilot's quantity is said as « a thread of diesel ».** R4 refuses it. What stays is a bench result: the diesel's kilograms an hour at the three points of Table 3.13, and the power, 51 kW against 64.
- **The process ratios stay** (air per kilo of wood, equivalence ratio, air for one of gas, the stack's drawn height): they are published physics with their marks and their sources, R3 and R4 list none of them, and none is a slider.
- **The disposal sentences are gone** from the condensate barrel and the ditch (R7): what the liquid holds, its ratio to the wartime sewer limit and « ask your local authority » stay; the ditch is there because no source says where the black water went.
- **The carbon monoxide warning is said in full three times**: the notice on the way in, the garage's overview, the Sources panel. The farm's overview names it in one different line.
- **« Not to scale » is said of what is not**: the journey slider and sheets PL-G03 and PL-G05 put the zones in order; the section of the gasifier's world is to scale.
- **The ladder's line in the Simulation names no time** (`Rung.plain`); the sheet PL-G08 keeps the published sentences with theirs.
- **The strip's alarms are short, and two are the panel's** (`STRIP_ALARMS`, `PANEL_ONLY` in `src/panels/strings.ts`): at 1280 px the kit's strip leaves the alarms what its values leave, so the garage shows two figures (`GARAGE_VALUES` in `src/panels/strip.ts`: the carbon monoxide of the two rooms) and the alarms a visitor can reach stand together, the engine's gas, a rung of the ladder, the room above and the lid's leak (about 610 px in French against about 650). The door's remark and the starting fan's chip (205 px) stay in the panel, whole. A test walks the reachable states and holds the characters of the chips together. « CO au-dessus » and « Symptômes publiés » stay as they are: the longer forms the fact-check suggests do not fit that budget.
- **The French name of the house is « Maison »** (« Ferme » was also the button of the first world).

## Departures from the research

- **§0.5 and §4.6, the guides.** A kit step plays in one world, so the steps that touched two worlds (the bunker and the unit's lid, the fan and the engine bay) are cut apart; four steps of the assembly carry a warning (the bunker, the lid, the fan, the engine bay) and one of the installation (the stop). The first step of each says it is not a procedure.
- **§0.6, the sheets.** Ten, not the eight of the standard (the research's plan); numbered group by group because the kit lists them so; sheet 4 is drawn against the journey's cursor and not a height (R2); sheet 10 is totals only.
- **§0.6, the lessons.** Seven, not six, because the stop and the room deserved their own: the second.
