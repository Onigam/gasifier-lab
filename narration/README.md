# Narration scripts (French and English)

`fr.json` and `en.json` follow `lab-kit/docs/narration.md`: **73 segments each** (`intro`, 5 worlds, 67 parts: farm 10, tractor 13, gasifier 20, gas line 17, garage 7), ids `intro`, `scene-<world>`, `part-<id>`, no lesson segments. The audio is in the **local voice** (`--engine voicebox`, profiles `Onigam narrateur FR` and `EN`, 0 credit, no ElevenLabs), in `public/audio/<lang>/` (10 to 11 MB a language, 73 mp3 and a manifest): see « Generated and checked » below. **Nobody has listened to it**: what is measured is below, and what could only be heard is said there.

**Four passes.** The first (2026-11-11) wrote the scripts and made the audio. The second, the same day, corrected the sentences that the fact-check (`docs/fact-check.md` §2) and the review (`docs/qa.md`) found going past their sources or breaking the lab's refusals, in both scripts, and made again 34 clips in each language. A **third, small pass** (after the second fact-check pass) changed five segments of each language: the gasifier world's line on what is not to scale, the farm garage's electrical faults, and three « no source says » made « no source we read says ». A **fourth pass**, the owner's « moins d'AI slop », rewrote 52 segments of each language after the audit of `studio/narration-style` (the stock opener, rhetorical questions as hinges, « not X, it is Y », announced honesty, kickers, exclamations, « voici » and « voyez », the lab as an actor): every figure, hedge and refusal is kept, and `scripts/slop-check.mjs` now holds the style (see « The slop check » below). What changed, pass by pass, is in `docs/narration-notes.md`; `src/narration.test.ts` keeps the old wordings out of the scripts and the corrections in.

`npm run build` runs `scripts/check-narration.mjs` first: every segment of a manifest needs its file, and a manifest and its script must name the same segments. `npm test` holds the scripts (`src/narration.test.ts`), the manifests and the guards of the refused sizes.

Check the length without generating anything (`HOME=<empty dir>` keeps the tool from even opening a key file; after a change of text the dry run names the clips to make again, and « 0 to generate » says that nothing is stale):

```
HOME=$(mktemp -d) node ~/Dev/lab-kit/tools/narration/generate.mjs --src narration/fr.json --out public/audio/fr --dry-run --engine voicebox
HOME=$(mktemp -d) node ~/Dev/lab-kit/tools/narration/generate.mjs --src narration/en.json --out public/audio/en --dry-run --engine voicebox
```

## Register

Pelton Lab's tone (`lab-kit/docs/narration-style.md`). The thread is **one wood chip**, as the research asked: it is stored in the barn, cut by the chipper's knives, rides the roof rack, sinks for an hour through the four zones, and is left as a little ash while its gas goes on to the engine; it is a subject, introduced with a fact (« Une plaquette de bois est stockée au sec, dans la grange »), never a guide to follow. It is in the introduction and in the gasifier's world; the parts keep to what each does, a surprise first and a concrete detail last (`injection-pump`: the gasifier is hot; `throat-ring`: « pas comment la faire »; `old-rescue-set`: « environ dix mille cas chroniques enregistrés en Suède en cinq ans »). The introduction opens on a fact (« Un soir de juin, dans le sud de la Suède, il y a plus d'un demi-siècle, un tracteur rentre des champs. Il roule au bois, avec un filet de gazole. ») and closes on the Sources panel, not on an invitation: the last world is about a gas that kills.

**What the voice does not say, by the lead's rules.** The carbon monoxide warning, the emergency numbers and the first-aid line are not in the voice at all: the entry notice, the Sources panel and the garage's overview say them, once each. No sentence tells how to build, light, run or make safe a gasifier, and `OPERATING_FR` / `OPERATING_EN` of `src/data/lint.ts` are run on every spoken sentence. The words that promise (`PROMISES`) and the sentences of the sources the lab does not repeat (`NOT_REPEATED`) are held off too. The pilot's share of diesel is not said either (research R4; the review's D2): the voice says that a thread of diesel stays, and that wood alone is not enough.

**The model's figures.** « 34 kg an hour before 41 »: every segment that says the bench's forty-one says the model's thirty-four before it, and a test holds the order (the introduction gives the bench's efficiency and its input as « par le calcul » without the 41 for that reason). The voice never says that the model retrieves the machine: it says « L'écart vient du modèle : son moteur brûle mieux que celui du banc, et son gazogène aussi », the reason as two. It never says the model's flame goes out (a test holds every « s'éteint » and « goes out » to the lamp that never does, and to the flame that never does). « Le modèle » is in 19 segments of 73, where a figure stands against the bench's or the book's, never on every figure (a test holds 15 to 36).

**What the sources said and the voice says as theirs.** A figure is attributed to the source that gives it and at the strength it gives it: « les Suédois » for what the Swedish book judged, « le rapport » for the report, « un autre manuel » and « une source américaine » for the others, « typiquement » for what a source says of gasifiers in general, « sur le camion du programme » for what was measured on the truck. « Souvent, selon le livre » for the fires' electrical faults (the book says « most » with a reserve, which the sheet states; the voice says « often » and gives no percentage), and « aucune source lue » where the lab says that no source says: it has read a few, and the Swedish originals are unread. What is the model's is said to be (« dans le modèle », « ce choix est le sien ; aucune mesure ne le donne », « le modèle lit ce chiffre ainsi »).

**Doors.** Nowhere does the voice say that a door, an open window or the open air helps. Where the open-door cue runs (`scene-garage`, `garage-door`), the Swedes' verdict is spoken before the cue, as what it was: it is said of fanning and starting with the garage doors open (S3 pp. 311–312), not of a parked car's post-gassing, and the voice adds that the engine is stopped here. The figure that falls is said to be what a well-mixed box gives (`scene-garage`), the open-door figure is introduced as « Porte ouverte » (`garage-door`), and a real garage is not a well-mixed box. A test refuses an open-door cue with no verdict before it.

**Anonymous.** « un institut suédois », « le rapport », « le livre suédois », « un autre manuel », « une source américaine »: no person, no company, no brand (the tractor, its engine and the truck are not named), no institution by its name, no city, no clinic, no treatment's name. A test refuses every name of the Sources panel and of the sheets (`NAMES` in `src/narration.test.ts`). The Sources panel says who said what, and the introduction says so once.

**The sizes of the hot parts.** None, in any language, and none hinted. The voice says what the throat ring, the nozzles, the fire box, the grate and the zones do, their lives (« sept cents heures », « six cents à mille heures », « quatre cents à sept cents heures », each attributed to the report) and their temperatures, and counts neither nozzles nor zones' heights. The gasifier's world says which things are not to scale (the journey's cursor and the gasifier's two drawings, the section and the journey's curves; the general arrangement is at 1:25) and that the section is: it gives no size. `src/data/spoken.ts` reads the number words of a script back as digits (« deux mètres vingt-deux » is « 2 m 22 »), and `src/data/docs-guard.test.ts` and `src/data/refusals.test.ts` hold every printed form of every refused length off both scripts, in both languages, and off any sentence that names a hot part and a length. Both guards were broken on purpose, once, to see them fail.

## The slop check (`scripts/slop-check.mjs`)

The owner asked for much less « AI slop » (2026-11-11). The audit of `studio/narration-style` (`STYLE.md`, `audit-gasifier.md`) names the tics; `scripts/slop-check.mjs` and `scripts/slop-patterns.json` are a copy of its checker (the lab cannot import from the studio). Per 1,000 characters it counts: the stock opener (P1), the signposts (P2), the short rhetorical question (P3), « not X, it is Y » (P4), announced honesty (P5), the kicker (P6), the personified object (P7), exclamations (P8, P9), the triplet and the anaphora (P10, P11), wonder-words, ellipsis beats and « et pourtant » (P12 to P14), « voici » and « voyez » (P15, P16), the closing formula (P17), the lab as an actor (P18), the question-and-fragment pair (P19), and, apart, the colons (P20), all questions (P21) and « ni X, ni Y » (P22). Each pattern has a ceiling (at least one hit is allowed); **the tic index** is the sum of P1 to P8, P10 to P14, P17 and P19: **0.13 per 1,000 characters in French, 0.22 in English** (it was 2.65 and 2.67 before the pass; Pelton Lab's own is 2.1; the target is 0.8).

`src/narration.test.ts` holds the index at 0.8 or less and no pattern over its ceiling in both languages, `scripts/check-narration.mjs` (the first step of `npm run build`) runs the same check and prints the index, and the test no longer asks for exclamations or questions (the kit's floors of 0.5 and 0.25 per 1,000 forced them): ceilings only. A regex is a tripwire, not a judge: the pass also read segments aloud (`docs/narration-notes.md`). To run it by hand: `node scripts/slop-check.mjs narration/fr.json --lang fr --list`.

## Spoken form

Numbers and units in words, « parties par million » for ppm, « mégajoules par mètre cube » and « kilojoules » in full, no symbol, no abbreviation, « à l'écran » for what the sheets call the 3D, acronyms replaced by words (« monoxyde de carbone », never CO). Estimates are said « environ » and « une estimation » where the sheets star them, and the model's results are said « le modèle » where a figure stands against the bench's or the book's; nothing is hedged twice. English: British spelling and units (metres, litres, tyres, storey, programme, grey, modelling), « per cent ».

## French and English

English is an adaptation: the same ids, the same cues at the matching place (a test holds every cue within a quarter of the segment of its French place), shorter by 5 per cent, and its openers are varied (« The » opens 14 of 73 segments, not 31). A few turns are English ones: « une pièce où l'on vit » is « a room where people live », the refusal of `throat-ring` is the same in both (« Ici, on dit ce que fait cette pièce, et rien de sa fabrication », « Here we say what this part does, and nothing of how it is made »), and a few French turns are shortened in English (« The grey far outweighs the work » for « Il y a bien plus de gris que de travail »). Nothing in the French is France-only, so nothing had to be made general: « voyez votre autorité locale » is « ask your local authority ». The second pass was made in both languages together, and the same sentences were corrected in each.

## Left out on purpose

See `docs/narration-notes.md`: the nine pellet-store deaths, the legal limits of carbon monoxide and the alarms' levels, the 17 deaths of 1939 to 1941 and the faulty gasifiers of 1945, the names of the old treatments and of the clinic, the casing's two losses as numbers, the sheets' lines on where the black water must not go. The fact-check has now read their sources, so none stands on a summary any more: they stay out for the budget and for the once-only rule, and can be added in the fact-check's wording. « Sometimes for hours » is in (`post-gas-plume`, as « parfois des heures selon un autre manuel »), without the manual's name.

**What nobody could check.** Nobody has listened. Things to listen for: « dans le lit chaud » was heard « dans le lycho » by Whisper in `scene-garage` (first pass) and in `part-post-gas-plume` (third pass), so the voice may run the three words together; « où qu'il aille » is heard « au quilaï » by both Whisper models in `fan-stack` (probably the faithful sound of a rare phrase, but a listener may not parse it); « sept leçons expliquent » in the introduction sounds like « cette leçon explique », and only « dix planches la dessinent » after it settles the plural; « lue ne » (reworded in the third pass) was the same kind of fault.
