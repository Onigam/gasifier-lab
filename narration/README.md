# Narration scripts (French and English)

`fr.json` and `en.json` follow `lab-kit/docs/narration.md`: **73 segments each** (`intro`, 5 worlds, 67 parts: farm 10, tractor 13, gasifier 20, gas line 17, garage 7), ids `intro`, `scene-<world>`, `part-<id>`, no lesson segments. The audio is in the **local voice** (`--engine voicebox`, profiles `Onigam narrateur FR` and `EN`, 0 credit, no ElevenLabs), in `public/audio/<lang>/` (11 MB a language, 73 mp3 and a manifest): see « Generated and checked » below. **Nobody has listened to it**: what is measured is below, and what could only be heard is said there.

**Two passes.** The first (2026-11-11) wrote the scripts and made the audio. The second, the same day, corrected the sentences that the fact-check (`docs/fact-check.md` §2) and the review (`docs/qa.md`) found going past their sources or breaking the lab's refusals, in both scripts, and made again the 34 clips in French and 34 in English whose text had changed. A **third, small pass** (the same day, after the second fact-check pass) changed five segments of each language: the gasifier world's line on what is not to scale, the farm garage's electrical faults, and three « no source says » made « no source we read says ». What changed, item by item, is in `docs/narration-notes.md`; `src/narration.test.ts` keeps the old wordings out of the scripts and the corrections in.

`npm run build` runs `scripts/check-narration.mjs` first: every segment of a manifest needs its file, and a manifest and its script must name the same segments. `npm test` holds the scripts (`src/narration.test.ts`), the manifests and the guards of the refused sizes.

Check the length without generating anything (`HOME=<empty dir>` keeps the tool from even opening a key file; after a change of text the dry run names the clips to make again, and « 0 to generate » says that nothing is stale):

```
HOME=$(mktemp -d) node ~/Dev/lab-kit/tools/narration/generate.mjs --src narration/fr.json --out public/audio/fr --dry-run --engine voicebox
HOME=$(mktemp -d) node ~/Dev/lab-kit/tools/narration/generate.mjs --src narration/en.json --out public/audio/en --dry-run --engine voicebox
```

## Register

Pelton Lab's tone (`lab-kit/docs/narration-style.md`). The thread is **one wood chip**, as the research asked: it is born under the chipper's knives, rides the roof rack, sinks for an hour through the four zones, and is left as a little ash while its gas goes on to the engine. It is in the introduction and in the gasifier's world; the parts keep to what each does, a surprise first and a concrete detail last (`injection-pump`: the gasifier is hot; `throat-ring`: « pas comment la faire »; `old-rescue-set`: « environ dix mille cas chroniques enregistrés en Suède en cinq ans »). The introduction opens on a tractor that « roule au bois, avec un filet de gazole » and closes on « une plaquette met une heure à descendre » and not on « à vous de jouer »: the last world is about a gas that kills.

**What the voice does not say, by the lead's rules.** The carbon monoxide warning, the emergency numbers and the first-aid line are not in the voice at all: the entry notice, the Sources panel and the garage's overview say them, once each. No sentence tells how to build, light, run or make safe a gasifier, and `OPERATING_FR` / `OPERATING_EN` of `src/data/lint.ts` are run on every spoken sentence. The words that promise (`PROMISES`) and the sentences of the sources the lab does not repeat (`NOT_REPEATED`) are held off too. The pilot's share of diesel is not said either (research R4; the review's D2): the voice says that a thread of diesel stays, and that wood alone is not enough.

**The model's figures.** « 34 kg an hour before 41 »: every segment that says the bench's forty-one says the model's thirty-four before it, and a test holds the order (the introduction gives the bench's efficiency and its input as « par le calcul » without the 41 for that reason). The voice never says that the model retrieves the machine: it says « pourquoi cet écart ? » and gives the reason as two, the engine's and the gasifier's. It never says the model's flame goes out (a test holds every « s'éteint » and « goes out » to the lamp that never does, and to the flame that never does). « Le modèle » is in 19 segments of 73, where a figure stands against the bench's or the book's, never on every figure (a test holds 15 to 36).

**What the sources said and the voice says as theirs.** A figure is attributed to the source that gives it and at the strength it gives it: « les Suédois » for what the Swedish book judged, « le rapport » for the report, « un autre manuel » and « une source américaine » for the others, « typiquement » for what a source says of gasifiers in general, « sur le camion du programme » for what was measured on the truck. « Souvent, selon le livre » for the fires' electrical faults (the book says « most » with a reserve, which the sheet states; the voice says « often » and gives no percentage), and « aucune source lue » where the lab says that no source says: it has read a few, and the Swedish originals are unread. What is the model's is said to be (« dans le modèle », « un choix du modèle, pas une mesure », « le modèle lit ce chiffre ainsi »).

**Doors.** Nowhere does the voice say that a door, an open window or the open air helps. Where the open-door cue runs (`scene-garage`, `garage-door`), the Swedes' verdict is spoken before the cue, as what it was: it is said of fanning and starting with the garage doors open (S3 pp. 311–312), not of a parked car's post-gassing, and the voice adds that the engine is stopped here. The figure that falls is then said to be what a well-mixed box is built to say. A test refuses an open-door cue with no verdict before it.

**Anonymous.** « un institut suédois », « le rapport », « le livre suédois », « un autre manuel », « une source américaine »: no person, no company, no brand (the tractor, its engine and the truck are not named), no institution by its name, no city, no clinic, no treatment's name. A test refuses every name of the Sources panel and of the sheets (`NAMES` in `src/narration.test.ts`). The Sources panel says who said what, and the introduction says so once.

**The sizes of the hot parts.** None, in any language, and none hinted. The voice says what the throat ring, the nozzles, the fire box, the grate and the zones do, their lives (« sept cents heures », « six cents à mille heures », « quatre cents à sept cents heures », each attributed to the report) and their temperatures, and counts neither nozzles nor zones' heights. The gasifier's world says which things are not to scale (the journey's cursor and the gasifier's two drawings, the section and the journey's curves; the general arrangement is at 1:25) and that the section is: it gives no size. `src/data/spoken.ts` reads the number words of a script back as digits (« deux mètres vingt-deux » is « 2 m 22 »), and `src/data/docs-guard.test.ts` and `src/data/refusals.test.ts` hold every printed form of every refused length off both scripts, in both languages, and off any sentence that names a hot part and a length. Both guards were broken on purpose, once, to see them fail.

## Cues (160 in each language, the same in both)

- Totals: `lab` 60 (preset 18, journey 22, scenario 12, speed 3, door 2, moisture, place and engine 1 each), frame 35, flows 26, home 11, xray 10, explode 6, mode 6, scene 5, select 1.
- Introduction (27): `home`, then the chip's trip: `frame` the barn and the chipper, `scene` to the tractor, `frame` the rack, `scene` to the gasifier with five `lab:journey` stops (drying, pyrolysis, flame, reduction, ash), `scene` to the gas line with two `frame`, `scene` to the garage with the engine's stop (`lab:scenario:engine-stops`) and `frame` the plume, the preset back, `scene` to the farm, the `mode` hops (assembly, installation, simulation, lessons, drawings, explore), `select:none`.
- Worlds (12, 13, 19, 10, 12): `home` first, one `frame` per element named (the garage frames four of its seven parts, the others have their own segment), `flows` where a path is told, `xray` and `explode` in the tractor's world only, `lab:journey` in the gasifier's. The farm uses no `xray` (it needs a selection there) and no `explode` (its tool is off); no world asks for a section, and none has one.
- Parts: no camera cue (a part starts framed and selected). `flows` on `gas-line-run`, `condensate-sleeve`, `air-nozzles`, `gas-outlet`, `hot-gas-pipe`, `primary-air-valve`, `gas-cooler`, `closed-garage`; `xray` on `cab`, `gasifier-unit`, `outer-casing`, `filter-box` (the parts that open their world's shell: a test refuses an X-ray on any other); `explode` on `tractor-chassis`, `eccentric-lock`.
- **The simulation follows the voice** (`src/cues.ts`): `lab:journey` on `chip-column`, `drying-zone`, `pyrolysis-zone`, `oxidation-zone`, `reduction-zone`, `ash-pit` (the cursor stands where the voice is); `lab:moisture:40` on `drying-zone`; `lab:scenario:air-leak` on `eccentric-lock`; `lab:scenario:filter-ageing` on `filter-bags` and `water-gauge`; `lab:scenario:idling` on `gas-throttle`; `lab:scenario:engine-stops` on `front-wheels` and `post-gas-plume`; `lab:scenario:closed-garage` on `closed-garage`, `garage-door` (with `lab:door:open`), `room-above` and `co-indicator` (with `lab:speed:600`); `lab:place:garage` and `lab:engine:off` on `exhaust-plume`.
- **The simulation is given back.** Every segment that touches it ends with `lab:preset:mf-1100` (the drawn machine, as the preset gives it) or leaves the journey on its own default (0.62): a test runs the cues of every segment on a real `GasifierSim` and compares its parameters with a fresh one's. `scene-gasifier`, `scene-gas-train`, `intro` and `exhaust-plume` also open with the preset, so that a visitor who had changed the run hears them on the machine they speak of. This also replaces what the visitor had set (the review's D7, kept as a request to the kit: restoring the visitor's own state at the end of a segment).
- Spacing: camera cues at least 35 spoken characters apart, counted on the spoken text as the generator counts it (cues out, runs of spaces made one). Everything an `xray`, `flows` or `explode` cue switches on is switched off before the end of its segment.

## Spoken form

Numbers and units in words, « parties par million » for ppm, « mégajoules par mètre cube » and « kilojoules » in full, no symbol, no abbreviation, « à l'écran » for what the sheets call the 3D, acronyms replaced by words (« monoxyde de carbone », never CO). Estimates are said « environ » and « une estimation » where the sheets star them, and the model's results are said « le modèle » where a figure stands against the bench's or the book's; nothing is hedged twice. English: British spelling and units (metres, litres, tyres, storey, programme, grey, modelling), « per cent ».

## French and English

English is an adaptation: the same ids, the same cues at the matching place (a test holds every cue within a quarter of the segment of its French place), shorter by 5 per cent. A few turns are English ones: « il mange du bois » is « it eats wood », « c'est l'histoire la plus grave de ce lab » is « the gravest story in this lab », « une pièce où l'on vit » is « a room where people live ». « Voyez » is « watch » or « see »; « le lab » is « the lab ». Nothing in the French is France-only, so nothing had to be made general: « voyez votre autorité locale » is « ask your local authority ». The second pass was made in both languages together, and the same sentences were corrected in each.

## Left out on purpose

See `docs/narration-notes.md`: the nine pellet-store deaths, the legal limits of carbon monoxide and the alarms' levels, the 17 deaths of 1939 to 1941 and the faulty gasifiers of 1945, the names of the old treatments and of the clinic, the casing's two losses as numbers, the sheets' lines on where the black water must not go. The fact-check has now read their sources, so none stands on a summary any more: they stay out for the budget and for the once-only rule, and can be added in the fact-check's wording. « Sometimes for hours » is in (`post-gas-plume`, as « parfois des heures selon un autre manuel »), without the manual's name.

**The probe.** `window.__lab.narration` (the kit's own engine, `expose` in dev) was driven in headless Chrome at 1280 by 800 on a dev server, in both languages, as in the first pass: for each segment the guide is played and paused, seeked to just after every cue with the clock held, and the world, the mode, the selected part, the X-ray, the explode, the flows and the simulation's own parameters are read and compared with what the cues ask (the simulation against a fresh `GasifierSim` given the last cue of each name), the captions are checked word for word against the manifest's, and the end of the segment is played out to see that nothing is left on and that the simulation is the drawn machine. **Result, both languages: 73 segments, 185 cue checks (the times just after each cue, and the start of each segment), 0 problems and no console error or warning.** Nothing is left on at the end of a segment, every part segment starts in its world with its part selected, and the simulation is the drawn machine at the end. One flag in the French run, on the introduction at the time of `mode:assembly`, was the probe's own: the assembly guide takes the camera to the gasifier's world by itself, which the probe now allows; the introduction was replayed clean. The probe needed the shared Chrome lock for about half an hour in all, held in turn with the other agents. After the third pass the five changed segments of each language were replayed again: 17 cue checks each, 0 problems, 0 console issues.

**What nobody could check.** Nobody has listened. One thing to listen for: « dans le lit chaud » is heard « dans le lycho » by Whisper in `scene-garage` (first pass) and in `part-post-gas-plume` (third pass), so the voice may run the three words together. « Lue ne » was the same kind of fault, and the three French sentences that had it were reworded (« rien de ce que nous avons lu ne… »). Also not checked: the pronunciation of « tuyères », « goudrons », « déchiqueteuse », « kilojoules », « gasifier », « phenols », and of the words the second pass added (« typiquement », « granulés », « typically », « pellets », « haulage »); the rhythm of the pauses at « … »; whether the voice sounds like the other labs' narrator; whether a subtitle falls on the word it shows. Whisper hears words, not intonation, and the voice may stress a number or the end of a sentence in a way no transcript shows. The camera's flights and the simulation's motion were looked at on a few screenshots in the first pass, not on a real device.
