# Narration scripts (French and English)

`fr.json` and `en.json` follow `lab-kit/docs/narration.md`: **73 segments each** (`intro`, 5 worlds, 67 parts: farm 10, tractor 13, gasifier 20, gas line 17, garage 7), ids `intro`, `scene-<world>`, `part-<id>`, no lesson segments. The audio is in the **local voice** (`--engine voicebox`, profiles `Onigam narrateur FR` and `EN`, 0 credit, no ElevenLabs), in `public/audio/<lang>/` (10 to 11 MB a language, 73 mp3 and a manifest): see « Generated and checked » below. **Nobody has listened to it**: what is measured is below, and what could only be heard is said there.

**Seven passes.** The first (2026-11-11) wrote the scripts and made the audio. The second, the same day, corrected the sentences that the fact-check (`docs/fact-check.md` §2) and the review (`docs/qa.md`) found going past their sources or breaking the lab's refusals, in both scripts, and made again 34 clips in each language. A **third, small pass** (after the second fact-check pass) changed five segments of each language: the gasifier world's line on what is not to scale, the farm garage's electrical faults, and three « no source says » made « no source we read says ». A **fourth pass**, the owner's « moins d'AI slop », rewrote 52 segments of each language after the audit of `studio/narration-style` (the stock opener, rhetorical questions as hinges, « not X, it is Y », announced honesty, kickers, exclamations, « voici » and « voyez », the lab as an actor): every figure, hedge and refusal is kept, and `scripts/slop-check.mjs` now holds the style (see « The slop check » below). A **fifth pass** (2026-11-12) answered the fact-check's fourth pass (`docs/fact-check.md`, F1 to F9 and C1 to C6) and the review's fourth (`docs/qa.md`, S1 to S17): 49 segments of the French and 47 of the English changed. Each part then said in a few words which source it quotes (the seventh pass, below, took most of those names out again), the introduction introduces the institute and its report once, the model's box and the Swedes' verdict are said once each in the garage world, the door has its three states, the model's 55 has its unit, and the listening hazards are reworded. A **sixth pass** (2026-11-12) answered the fact-check's fifth pass (and the review's fifth: the garage door's figures are said over a strip that shows them, the indicator's needle is followed, « Porte entrouverte », three subtitle cuts) (`docs/fact-check.md`: B1, B2, M1, M2 and m1 to m11): the report explains the baffle in its text and gives the headlights' reason, so the voice says both as the report's (the fourth pass's « sans un mot » and « Notre lecture » were wrong); the stop's gas forms « dans le combustible encore chaud », not in a bed; the secondary-air throttle worked by hand is the programme's truck's; the report's general chapters, the vessel's intervals, the thirty per cent and the pump's seizure are given back to their scope. 19 segments of each language changed (and three of them again after the fact-check's sixth pass: the pump's shield « contre la chaleur du gazogène », the thirty per cent as the report's, the spring lid's superlative cut), and the test's upper limit stays 25,000. A **seventh pass** (2026-11-12) did what the owner decided after the review's D-N3: « le rapport sur l'institut » (17 segments) and « le livre suédois sur les gazogènes » (9) were a tic when heard in a row, so a published fact is now said as a plain statement with its figure, the full formula is spoken once per world per source at most (4 times for the report, once for the book, in the whole guide; 18 and 8 before), and a short « le rapport », « le livre », « les Suédois », « un autre manuel » stays only where the source is silent, where a hedge was pinned, where the source and the model sit side by side or where a verdict is the source's: 6 short forms, in 11 segments that name a source (35 before; 12 with the introduction). 28 segments of each language were redone (the introduction is untouched), and `src/narration.test.ts` fails above the ceilings (« names a source only where the naming does work »). A **correction round** followed the fact-check's and the review's seventh passes (`docs/fact-check.md` M1, M2, m1, m2, m4; `docs/qa.md` D-N10 to D-N17): 12 segments of each language were made again (`room-above` with « En Suède » and the 670 as the model's and read on the strip at the instant it is said, `injection-pump` with the report's « attributed to », the two exhaust clips, `throat-ring`, `filter-bags`, `secondary-air-throttle`, `left-consoles`, `primary-air-valve`, `cyclone`, `roof-headlights`, `farm-garage`), and the guard now compares sources, not forms, and counts the wider markers (the bench, a test truck, several vehicles, readings). ## Register

Pelton Lab's tone (`lab-kit/docs/narration-style.md`). The thread is **one wood chip**, as the research asked: it is stored in the barn, cut by the chipper's knives, rides the roof rack, sinks for an hour through the four zones, and is left as a little ash while its gas goes on to the engine; it is a subject, introduced with a fact (« Une plaquette de bois est stockée au sec, dans la grange »), never a guide to follow. It is in the introduction and in the gasifier's world; the parts keep to what each does, a surprise first and a concrete detail last (`injection-pump`: the gasifier is hot; `throat-ring`: « pas comment la faire »; `old-rescue-set`: « environ dix mille cas chroniques ont été enregistrés en Suède en cinq ans »). The introduction opens on a setting marked as imagined (« Un matin de juin imaginé, dans le sud de la Suède, il y a plus d'un demi-siècle : un tracteur part aux champs. Il roule au bois, avec un filet de gazole. »), introduces the Swedish institute and its report once, with what the eighteen per cent is of, and closes on the Sources panel, not on an invitation: the last world is about a gas that kills.

**What the voice does not say, by the lead's rules.** The carbon monoxide warning, the emergency numbers and the first-aid line are not in the voice at all: the entry notice, the Sources panel and the garage's overview say them, once each. No sentence tells how to build, light, run or make safe a gasifier, and `OPERATING_FR` / `OPERATING_EN` of `src/data/lint.ts` are run on every spoken sentence. The words that promise (`PROMISES`) and the sentences of the sources the lab does not repeat (`NOT_REPEATED`) are held off too. The pilot's share of diesel is not said either (research R4; the review's D2): the voice says that a thread of diesel stays, and that wood alone is not enough.

**The model's figures.** « 34 kg an hour before 41 »: every segment that says the bench's forty-one says the model's thirty-four before it, and a test holds the order (the introduction gives the bench's efficiency and its input as « par le calcul » without the 41 for that reason). The voice never says that the model retrieves the machine: it says « L'écart vient du modèle : son moteur brûle mieux que celui du banc, et son gazogène aussi », the reason as two. It never says the model's flame goes out (a test holds every « s'éteint » and « goes out » to the lamp that never does, and to the flame that never does). « Le modèle » is in 21 segments of 73, where a figure stands against the bench's or the book's, never on every figure (a test holds 15 to 36).

**What the sources said and the voice says as theirs.** A published figure is said as a plain statement with its figure, and the introduction says once that an unmarked figure on a sheet is published, that the model's own are said « le modèle … », and where the published ones come from (« ce rapport », « un livre suédois sur les gazogènes », « des manuels »); the sheet's source tag and the Sources panel carry the rest. A source is named in a segment only where the naming does work (the seventh pass, `docs/narration-notes.md` §1f): where it is silent (« Le rapport ne dit pas… » in `roof-rack`, `left-consoles` and `stop-valve`; « Rien de ce que nous avons lu ne dit… » in `cyclone`, `front-wheels`, `cab`, `farmhouse`, `field-ditch` and `post-gas-plume`: the lab saying what it does not know, which stays), where a hedge was pinned by a fact-check (« jugé acceptable », « parfois des heures selon un autre manuel », « une source américaine »: the hot ash is not the Swedish book's; « aurait été caché » and « souvent, semble-t-il » keep their hedge in the verb and the adverb, the sheets name the report and the book), where a verdict is the source's (« Les Suédois ont jugé très insuffisant… », before every open-door cue), where the book ranks the worst accidents (`farmhouse`; `room-above` says « En Suède » and names no one), and where a diagram is the report's (`primary-air-valve`: « Sur le schéma du rapport »). The full formulas (« le rapport sur l'institut », « le livre suédois sur les gazogènes ») are said once per world per source at most, in the first segment of the world that needs them: the report in the farm (`chip-barn`), the tractor (`roof-rack`), the gasifier (`fuel-baffle`) and the gas line (`stop-valve`), the book in the farm (`farmhouse`); « un camion d'essai » says that a figure is a truck's and not this tractor's, with no name (the report has two trucks, and the voice says nothing that makes them one). What is a scope stays said: « d'après plusieurs véhicules » (`fire-box`, `grate`, `throat-ring`), « un bac de ce genre », « en général », « des relevés donnent, à l'échappement des moteurs de la guerre » (a reading, no author). `injection-pump` says that the seizure « a été attribué à un manque de refroidissement et de graissage »: the report's reading, not the lab's cause. « Aucune source lue » is « rien de ce que nous avons lu » where the lab says that no source says (it has read a few, the Swedish originals are unread). What is the model's is said to be (« dans le modèle », « ce choix est celui du modèle ; aucune mesure ne le donne », « le modèle lit ce chiffre ainsi »).

**Doors.** Nowhere does the voice say that a door, an open window or the open air helps. Where the open-door cue runs (`scene-garage`, `garage-door`), the Swedes' verdict is spoken before the cue, as what it was: it is said of the starting fan and the engine with the garage doors open (S3 pp. 311–312), not of a parked car's post-gassing, and the voice adds that the engine is stopped here. ## The slop check (`scripts/slop-check.mjs`)

The owner asked for much less « AI slop » (2026-11-11). The audit of `studio/narration-style` (`STYLE.md`, `audit-gasifier.md`) names the tics; `scripts/slop-check.mjs` and `scripts/slop-patterns.json` are a copy of its checker (the lab cannot import from the studio). Per 1,000 characters it counts: the stock opener (P1), the signposts (P2), the short rhetorical question (P3), « not X, it is Y » (P4), announced honesty (P5), the kicker (P6), the personified object (P7), exclamations (P8, P9), the triplet and the anaphora (P10, P11), wonder-words, ellipsis beats and « et pourtant » (P12 to P14), « voici » and « voyez » (P15, P16), the closing formula (P17), the lab as an actor (P18), the question-and-fragment pair (P19), and, apart, the colons (P20), all questions (P21) and « ni X, ni Y » (P22). Each pattern has a ceiling (at least one hit is allowed); **the tic index** is the sum of P1 to P8, P10 to P14, P17 and P19: **0.12 per 1,000 characters in French, 0.21 in English** (it was 2.65 and 2.67 before the pass; Pelton Lab's own is 2.1; the target is 0.8).

`src/narration.test.ts` holds the index at 0.8 or less and no pattern over its ceiling in both languages, `scripts/check-narration.mjs` (the first step of `npm run build`) runs the same check and prints the index, and the test no longer asks for exclamations or questions (the kit's floors of 0.5 and 0.25 per 1,000 forced them): ceilings only. A regex is a tripwire, not a judge: the pass also read segments aloud (`docs/narration-notes.md`). To run it by hand: `node scripts/slop-check.mjs narration/fr.json --lang fr --list`.

## Spoken form

Numbers and units in words, « parties par million » for ppm, « mégajoules par mètre cube » and « kilojoules » in full, no symbol, no abbreviation, « à l'écran » for what the sheets call the 3D, acronyms replaced by words (« monoxyde de carbone », never CO). Estimates are said « environ » and « une estimation » where the sheets star them, and the model's results are said « le modèle » where a figure stands against the bench's or the book's; nothing is hedged twice. English: British spelling and units (metres, litres, tyres, storey, programme, grey, modelling), « per cent ».

## French and English

English is an adaptation: the same ids, the same cues at the matching place (a test holds every cue within a quarter of the segment of its French place), shorter by 5 per cent, and its openers are varied (« The » opens 14 of 73 segments, not 31). A few turns are English ones: « une pièce où l'on vit » is « a room where people live », the refusal of `throat-ring` is the same in both (« Ici, on dit ce que fait cette pièce, et rien de sa fabrication », « Here we say what this part does, and nothing of how it is made »), and a few French turns are shortened in English (« The grey far outweighs the work » for « Il y a bien plus de gris que de travail »). Nothing in the French is France-only, so nothing had to be made general: « voyez votre autorité locale » is « ask your local authority ». The second pass was made in both languages together, and the same sentences were corrected in each.
